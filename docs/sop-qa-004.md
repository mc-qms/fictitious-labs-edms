# SOP-QA-004 — Change Control

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how changes to processes, systems, equipment, materials, and documents are proposed, assessed, approved, implemented, and verified, so that no change is made without evaluation of its impact on product quality.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all changes affecting validated processes, qualified equipment, approved materials, specifications, and controlled documents at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory, Manufacturing.

Does not cover emergency changes made to protect patients or product during an ongoing event, which follow the deviation procedure and are assessed retrospectively.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Requester**
  - Describe the proposed change, its rationale, and the affected processes, systems, equipment, materials, or documents.
  - Provide relevant background information and identify known potential impacts.

- **Change Owner**
  - Coordinate the assessment and ensure the change's scope and implications are evaluated.
  - Coordinate required studies, implementation, and post-implementation verification. The Change Owner is assigned by Quality Assurance from the function that owns the affected process, system, or document. The Change Owner coordinates the change but does not approve it.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Review the change assessment and confirm that product-quality, patient-safety, and validated-state impacts have been evaluated before approval.
  - Approve or reject changes within the Quality Unit's authority.
  - Quality Assurance classifies every change as Major or Minor under Section 4.3, and that classification is final. Minor changes are approved by the Change Owner and Quality Assurance; Major changes also require the concurrence of the QA Manager.

- **Approvers**
  - Review the change and its assessment, then approve or reject it within their assigned authority. Do not approve a change for implementation until applicable regulatory requirements for the change have been met. Approvers are the owners of the functions the change affects, together with Quality Assurance; Quality Assurance assigns the approvers when the change is classified.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Propose the change

1. The Requester shall describe the proposed change and its rationale.
2. Identify the affected product, process, system, equipment, material, specification, or controlled document.
3. The Requester and Change Owner shall identify the change's scope and relevant background information, including known interactions with other changes or parts of the control strategy.
4. Change requests are submitted in FL-EDMS; Document Control assigns the change request number (format CR-<year>-<number>, e.g., CR-2026-007). A submission states the description and rationale, the affected products, processes, systems, equipment, materials, specifications, and documents, and the known potential impacts.
5. Do not use this procedure for an emergency change made to protect patients or product during an ongoing event. Handle that event under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations, and assess the change retrospectively.

### 4.2 Assess the change

1. Before approval, assess every change for its impact on product quality, patient safety, and the validated state. Make the depth of the assessment proportionate to the risk.
2. The Change Owner shall coordinate a science- and knowledge-based assessment. Consider the potential consequences of the change and of not making it, relevant uncertainty and assumptions, and whether the available evidence is sufficient to support the decision.
3. Assess impacts on affected products and processes, the control strategy, specifications, materials, equipment, systems, and controlled documents. Consider concurrent changes and whether risk controls introduce new risks or increase existing risks.
4. For equipment, facility, or utility changes, assess relevant design and product-contact considerations, qualification scope, calibration, and preventive maintenance. For computer systems or computer-controlled equipment, assess software and hardware design, intended use, validation needs, critical performance, and electronic record and signature reliability.
5. For manufacturing-process changes, determine the needed verification, qualification, or validation, including follow-up monitoring or sampling where supported by the assessment. Consider supplier or contract-manufacturer impacts and variability in starting materials.
6. Determine what existing or new data, tests, or studies are needed to support the change.
7. Where studies are needed, define their methods and acceptance criteria before execution. The site's risk-assessment method is the Major/Minor classification of Section 4.3, supported by the documented science- and risk-based rationale of this section; a change is acceptable only when Quality Assurance determines that the residual risk to product quality is acceptable with the planned controls in place.
8. If the change affects a specification, standard, sampling plan, test procedure, or other laboratory control, the organizational unit responsible for that laboratory control drafts the change — for laboratory controls, the Quality Control laboratory.
9. Quality Assurance shall review and approve the change.

### 4.3 Determine the approval and regulatory route

1. Quality Assurance classifies the change: **Major** — the change could affect product quality, safety, or a regulatory filing or established condition; **Minor** — no such effect. Quality Assurance's classification is final and is recorded in the change record.
2. Quality Assurance assesses whether the change affects any regulatory filing or established condition. A change to an established condition requires a regulatory submission; do not classify such a change as not reported.
3. Quality Assurance determines the regulatory communication category under FDA's post-approval change requirements for licensed biological products.
4. Quality Assurance prepares and submits any required communication.
5. Quality Assurance confirms that any required prior approval has been received. Do not implement a change that requires prior regulatory approval until that approval has been received. If a change affects a regulatory filing or established condition, it is Major and is not implemented until Quality Assurance confirms the filing impact is addressed.
6. The Approvers shall review the assessment and the proposed implementation conditions before deciding whether to approve or reject the change. The approval sequence is: Change Owner, then the approvers of the affected functions, then Quality Assurance, with the QA Manager also approving Major changes.
7. Record the decision and its basis in the change record. A rejection, or a request for further information before a decision, is recorded in the change record with its reasons. Do not implement a rejected change.
8. Changes managed within the quality system still require assessment, approval, documentation, and verification under this procedure, even when no regulatory report is required.

### 4.4 Implement the change

1. Implement an approved change only after its required quality-system approvals and any required prior regulatory approval have been obtained.
2. Before implementation, complete the studies, tests, qualification, validation, document updates, training, and other controls identified as required before implementation in the approved assessment.
3. Implementation is ready when every pre-implementation item in the approved assessment is complete; the Change Owner confirms readiness and Quality Assurance concurs, and the confirmation is recorded in the change record.
4. Implement the change in accordance with the approved assessment and supporting protocols. If results fail acceptance criteria, conditions are not met, or unanticipated risks arise, do not proceed under the approved plan.
5. The Change Owner shall coordinate reassessment of the change.
6. Quality Assurance decides the revised controls and regulatory route — with the QA Manager, for a Major change — and Quality Assurance authorizes implementation to resume.
7. Obtain the required approvals and meet applicable regulatory requirements before further implementation.
8. Communicate approved changes to affected personnel and relevant manufacturing stakeholders. Ensure that Quality Assurance, which is responsible for batch release, is aware of all relevant changes.
9. Quality Assurance determines whether a change requires disposition of in-process or released lots; the trigger for that determination is any change affecting an in-process or released lot, or the release process itself.

### 4.5 Verify and close the change

1. After implementation, assess the generated data against the change objective and applicable acceptance criteria recorded in the approved assessment or study protocol.
2. Record the outcome in the change record in FL-EDMS.
3. Verify whether the change achieved its intended outcome without unintended consequences for product quality.
4. Record the verification results and any new product or process knowledge.
5. If verification identifies a deviation, failed criterion, or unanticipated risk, manage the issue through SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations.
6. Identify and address corrective or preventive actions through SOP-QA-011, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA).
7. Quality Assurance determines whether regulatory filings or other change records need updating as a result of the change, and completes those updates.
8. For a Major change, Quality Assurance also completes the post-implementation review at the date set in the change record.
9. Quality Assurance closes the change in FL-EDMS when implementation is verified, required filing and record updates are complete, and any post-implementation review is done.

## 5. Records

Change records (CR-<year>-<number>), including the request, assessment, classification, approvals, implementation and verification records, and closure, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
