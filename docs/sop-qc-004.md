# SOP-QC-004 — Cell Bank Management

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Control

## 1. Purpose

Establish how master and working cell banks are created, characterized, stored, and issued, so that production always starts from a qualified, traceable cell substrate.

## 2. Scope

Applies to cell banks used in the manufacture of products at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Control laboratory, Manufacturing, Quality Assurance.

Does not cover cell line development and clone selection research before banking.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Cell Bank Operator**
  - Prepare and handle cell banks, maintain bank and cell-age traceability, and record banking activities and results.

- **Laboratory Supervisor**
  - Coordinate cell-bank characterization and testing, review the associated technical records, and ensure that unresolved results are escalated.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Review the proposed banking and qualification approach, assess unresolved quality issues, and determine whether the bank may be used for production.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Plan the banking strategy

1. Describe the banking strategy, anticipated production use, qualification criteria for each bank, and intervals for generating new banks.
2. The banking strategy, qualification criteria, and bank-generation intervals for each bank are defined in the bank qualification protocol approved by Quality Assurance before banking; a new working cell bank is generated when projected inventory falls below two years of production supply or as the protocol directs.
3. Use a two-tier system in which the master cell bank (MCB) supplies working cell banks (WCBs), and WCBs supply production. Fictitious Labs uses the two-tier system; a single-tier MCB system is not used.
4. Define the in vitro cell-age limit for production and how cell age will be measured from thaw of the MCB container or containers through harvest of the production vessel.
5. The cell-age limit, the measurement method, and any senescence limit are established in the qualification protocol. The FL-101 production line is a CHO-derived line and is not a diploid line, so no senescence limit applies to it; for any diploid line, the population-doubling level at which senescence occurs is established in the protocol before use.

### 4.2 Prepare the MCB

1. Prepare the MCB from the selected clone or another justified cell source under defined conditions. The FL-101 MCB is prepared from the selected production clone under the conditions of the approved banking protocol.
2. Combine cells from all culture vessels into one pooled suspension before filling the bank containers.
3. Define.
4. Record the bank size, container and closure, culture medium, cryoprotectant, preservation method, and storage conditions. These parameters are those of the approved banking protocol.
5. Use sealed, sterilized containers and storage conditions that maintain consistent viability adequate for production after recovery. Banks are held in sealed cryovials in vapor-phase liquid nitrogen at ≤ −130 °C; the post-thaw viability acceptance criterion is set in the banking protocol.
6. Label each container so its identity and contents remain traceable through preservation, storage, and recovery.
7. Each container label carries the bank ID, container number, cell line, freeze date, and storage location, and bank traceability is maintained in the bank inventory in FL-EDMS.
8. Apply controls that prevent microbial contamination and cell-line cross-contamination during banking. Banking is performed under SOP-QA-024, Aseptic Processing and Media Fills, with only one cell line handled in a suite at a time and line clearance between lines.
9. Define measures to protect banks from events that could make them unusable, such as loss of storage conditions or power.
10. MCB containers are divided between two independent liquid-nitrogen freezers in separate locations, with continuous temperature monitoring and alarms; a storage failure is escalated under section 4.5.

### 4.3 Derive WCBs

1. Start each WCB from one or more MCB containers and culture the cells under defined conditions. The MCB containers for each WCB are selected as the approved banking protocol directs.
2. Prepare the WCB from a homogeneous cell suspension.
3. Dispense it into multiple containers. WCB culture, harvest, pooling, dispensing, and preservation conditions are those of the approved WCB protocol.
4. Record the MCB container or containers used to start the WCB.
5. Maintain traceability to the resulting WCB containers.
6. Characterize and test each new WCB before production use. WCB qualification criteria are those of the qualification protocol.
7. Quality Assurance authorizes each new WCB for production use; the authorization is recorded in FL-EDMS.

### 4.4 Characterize and test the banks

1. Test each MCB for identity and purity.
2. Test cell stability during cultivation once for each product.
3. Characterize each WCB for purity and limited identity. Methods and acceptance criteria are the qualified methods and criteria listed in the bank testing protocol approved by Quality Assurance.
4. For banks of metazoan cells, test individual containers for bacteria and fungi using 1% of the total container count, but test at least two containers.
5. Test for mycoplasma. Where 1% of the total container count is fractional, the count is rounded up to the next whole container.
6. Establish a virus-testing strategy based on assessment of cell-line origin and history, materials used to generate, bank, and culture the cells, and relevant virus risks. Include broad and specific assays as warranted by that assessment. Consider assay sensitivity, specificity, and possible matrix interference.
7. The risk assessment, selected assays, and acceptance criteria are documented in the qualification protocol by Quality Control.
8. Approved by Quality Assurance.
9. Test the MCB for endogenous and adventitious viruses.
10. Test WCBs for adventitious viruses.
11. Test production cells at the limit of in vitro cell age (LIVCA) for viruses. Virus testing is performed on the MCB; the alternative of completing testing on the WCB instead of the MCB is not used.
12. Expand LIVCA cells from the MCB or WCB using a representative production-cell model, at small scale and/or at pilot-plant or commercial scale, to the proposed age or beyond.
13. Test these cells once for viruses that may not have been detected in the banks. The LIVCA study design and representative production-cell model are defined in the qualification protocol.
14. Characterize cell lines for retroviruses using testing appropriate to the line and its risk assessment. Testing may include infectivity, reverse-transcriptase activity, and transmission electron microscopy. For lines with unknown retrovirus-producing status, assess cells and clarified supernatant.
15. Investigate positive findings with infectivity testing in relevant permissive cells. The applicable tests, methods, and criteria for interpreting results are those of the bank testing protocol.
16. For in vitro infectivity testing, select indicator cells based on risk assessment, including cells from the species of origin, human diploid cells, and monkey kidney cells.
17. For MCB, WCB, and LIVCA qualification, test for 28 days with at least one sub-passage at two weeks, and monitor for cytopathic, hemadsorbing, and hemagglutinating viruses. Any additional indicator cells, test methods, and acceptance criteria are those of the bank testing protocol.
18. Select in vivo testing based on risk assessment. Where residual risk remains, retain the test or consider replacing it with non-targeted next-generation sequencing (NGS).
19. The decision to select or replace an in vivo test is made by Quality Assurance with the Laboratory Supervisor and documented in the qualification protocol.
20. If testing detects an adventitious virus at LIVCA, investigate the finding, including testing the MCB and WCB, to determine its source.
21. Quality Assurance assesses the investigation findings, and the MCB and WCB are placed on QA hold in FL-EDMS until the source is determined.
22. Assess production stability at a minimum of two points: minimal subcultivation and at or beyond the proposed in vitro cell-age limit.
23. Use production cells expanded under pilot-plant or commercial-scale conditions.
24. At or beyond that limit, verify relevant coding-sequence or product consistency.
25. Support any increase to the limit with data through the proposed new limit. The product-specific study design, methods, and acceptance criteria are those of the qualification protocol.

### 4.5 Store and monitor banks

1. Store each bank under conditions that maintain viability adequate for production after recovery.
2. Maintain traceability for each container through storage and recovery.
3. Establish a bank-stability monitoring proposal.
4. If production does not occur for an extended period, test viability at the interval specified for the product. If viability is not significantly decreased, further MCB or WCB testing is generally not necessary. The extended period is 24 months without production; viability is then tested at 24-month intervals against the criterion in the banking protocol.
5. Record.
6. Assess storage or monitoring results that could affect a bank's suitability for production.
7. An adverse result is escalated to the Laboratory Supervisor and Quality Assurance the same business day.
8. Quality Assurance decides the bank's suitability, and any product impact is handled under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations.

### 4.6 Issue banks for production

1. Issue only a bank that has met its defined qualification criteria and has been authorized for production use.
2. Quality Assurance authorizes issue, and the authorization is recorded in FL-EDMS.
3. Record the bank and container issued, its traceability information, and its intended production use. The issue record is held in FL-EDMS.
4. Start production cell-age tracking at thaw of the MCB container or containers, and track age through harvest of the production vessel using the defined measurement method.
5. Cell age is recorded in the batch record.
6. Verified by the QC reviewer against the applicable cell-age limit.

## 5. Records

Cell bank records, including banking and qualification protocols, bank inventory and traceability records, characterization and testing results, storage and monitoring records, authorizations, and issue records, retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
