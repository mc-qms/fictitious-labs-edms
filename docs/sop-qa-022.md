# SOP-QA-022 — Labeling and Packaging Control

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Manufacturing

## 1. Purpose

Establish how labeling and packaging materials are controlled, issued, reconciled, and verified, so that the right label is on the right product and no mix-up can pass undetected.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the receipt, storage, issuance, application, and reconciliation of labels and packaging materials at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Packaging and labeling, Quality Assurance, Manufacturing.

Does not cover artwork design and regulatory approval of label content, which follows the regulatory labeling procedures.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Packaging Operator**
  - Perform assigned material handling, issuance, line-clearance, labeling, in-process checking, and reconciliation steps.
  - Record each action when performed.
  - Stop the line if a labeling-count discrepancy is found.
  - Notify the Packaging Supervisor if a labeling-count discrepancy is found.

- **Packaging Supervisor**
  - Coordinate packaging operations and ensure required checks and records are completed.
  - Route discrepancies for investigation.
  - Authorize line restart after a corrected non-count discrepancy; restart after a labeling-count discrepancy requires Quality Assurance authorization.

- **Label Reviewer**
  - Check the labeling and associated batch records assigned for review: the identity and content of labeling issued for the batch against the Packaging Instructions and batch production record at issuance, the correctness of overprinting during packaging, and the labeling entries in the batch packaging record at completion.
  - Record the results of each check in the batch packaging record.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Review production records for errors.
  - Ensure errors are investigated.
  - Approve or reject packaging materials and labeling.
  - Decide whether a reconciliation discrepancy is satisfactorily accounted for before release.
  - Authorize restart after a labeling-count discrepancy.
  - Make the batch release decision under SOP-QA-006, Batch Record Review and Product Release.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Receive and assess materials

1. Receive each delivery of labeling and packaging materials under written procedures.
2. Record the material name, internal name or code if different, receipt date, supplier and manufacturer names, manufacturer's batch or reference number, total quantity and number of containers, assigned batch or control number, and relevant comments.
3. Check incoming materials against the order.
4. Examine containers for correct identification, damage, broken seals, and contamination.
5. Investigate damage or any other problem that could affect material quality before accepting or releasing the material.
6. Record damage or any other problem that could affect material quality before accepting or releasing the material.
7. Report damage or any other problem that could affect material quality to Quality Assurance before accepting or releasing the material.
8. Sample and examine or test labeling and packaging materials before use against the applicable written specifications.
9. Quality Control samples and examines or tests each material against its specification, under SOP-QA-015, Supplier Quality.
10. Quality Assurance releases each material for use, under SOP-QA-015, Supplier Quality.
11. Keep materials quarantined until the required examination or testing is complete and Quality Assurance releases them for use.
12. Identify each lot and its status.
13. Identify and control rejected materials to prevent their use. Rejected material carries Rejected status in FL-EDMS and is physically segregated in the quarantine area.
14. Only Quality Assurance dispositions rejected material, by destruction or return to the supplier.
15. Record receipt, examination or testing, and acceptance or rejection for each shipment of each labeling and packaging material in the material receipt record in FL-EDMS.

### 4.2 Store and control materials

1. Store printed materials securely to prevent unauthorized access.
2. Store labels and other labeling for each product, strength, dosage form, or quantity of contents separately with suitable identification.
3. Store cut labels and other loose printed materials in separate, closed containers during storage and transport.
4. Store materials under the conditions established for them and in an orderly way that supports batch segregation and stock rotation.
5. Use the oldest approved stock first.
6. Identify obsolete or outdated labeling and packaging materials.
7. Destroy obsolete or outdated labeling and packaging materials.
8. Record the destruction of obsolete or outdated labeling and packaging materials.
9. Printed labeling is stored in the access-controlled labeling storage area of the warehouse, under SOP-QA-023, Warehouse and Distribution Control, accessible only to authorized packaging and Quality Assurance personnel. Material status is shown in FL-EDMS and by physical status labeling, and storage conditions are those of the material specification.
10. Warehouse, with Quality Assurance, reviews stock periodically for obsolete or outdated materials.
11. Destruction is witnessed by Quality Assurance and recorded in FL-EDMS.

### 4.3 Issue materials for a batch

1. Issue packaging materials only through an approved, documented process and only through authorized personnel.
2. Before use, check the identity and quantity of each product and packaging material delivered to the packaging area against the approved Packaging Instructions.
3. Examine labeling issued for the batch to confirm that its identity and content conform to the labeling specified in the Packaging Instructions and batch production record. If these records do not agree, do not proceed with the labeling selection.
4. The batch production record governs: packaging does not proceed until Quality Assurance resolves the disagreement under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations.
5. Record the identity and quantity of materials issued for the batch in the batch packaging record. Issuance is performed by authorized warehouse or packaging personnel.
6. The Label Reviewer verifies identity and quantity at issuance.
7. Keep materials issued for each batch together and clearly identified as belonging to that batch.

### 4.4 Prepare the packaging line

1. Before packaging begins, clear the work area, line, printing equipment, and other equipment of products, materials, and documents from previous operations that are not required for the current batch.
2. Perform line clearance using the applicable checklist.
3. Document line clearance using the applicable checklist. Do not begin packaging until line clearance is complete and documented.
4. Line clearance uses the checklist in the batch packaging record, performed by the Packaging Operator.
5. Line clearance is verified and signed off by the Packaging Supervisor before packaging starts.
6. Display the product name and batch number at each packaging station or line.
7. Examine packaging and labeling materials for suitability and correctness before packaging starts.
8. Document the examination in the batch production record.

### 4.5 Apply labels and perform in-process checks

1. Use the packaging and labeling materials specified for the batch.
2. Keep different products physically separated during packaging. Do not package different products in close proximity unless physical segregation prevents mix-ups.
3. The Packaging Supervisor, with Quality Assurance, determines whether physical segregation prevents mix-ups; different products are not packaged at the same time on adjacent lines without a physical barrier.
4. Identify filled product containers held without labels so their product name, strength, quantity of contents, and lot or control number can be determined.
5. Check the correct performance of printing operations, including batch codes and expiry dates.
6. Record the correct performance of printing operations, including batch codes and expiry dates.
7. Recheck hand printing at regular intervals: hand printing is rechecked by a second person at least once per hour and whenever the print setup changes.
8. When electronic code readers, label counters, or similar devices are used, check that they operate correctly before use.
9. Each device is challenged with known correct and incorrect samples before use.
10. The result of each device challenge is recorded in the batch packaging record.
11. During packaging, check the general appearance and completeness of packages, correct product and packaging materials, correctness of overprinting, and proper functioning of line monitors.
12. Record these in-process results in the batch packaging record. Do not return samples removed from the packaging line to the line.
13. During finishing operations, examine packaged and labeled products to confirm that containers and packages in the lot have the correct label.
14. At completion of finishing operations, collect a representative sample of units and visually examine it for correct labeling.
15. Record the results of these examinations in the batch production or control records.
16. If cut labeling is used for immediate-container labels or cartons, apply one of these controls:
   - dedicate the labeling and packaging line to each product and strength;
   - use appropriate electronic or electromechanical equipment for a 100-percent examination of correct labeling;
   - for hand-applied labels, use a 100-percent visual examination by one person with an independent check by another person; or
   - use an automated technique that physically prevents incorrect labeling from being processed.

   The control selected for Fictitious Labs operations is 100-percent electronic examination where the line is fitted for it and, for hand-applied labels, 100-percent visual examination by one person with an independent check by a second person; the control in use is recorded in the batch's Packaging Instructions.
17. On an incorrect label, printing error, failed line monitor, or other failed in-process check: stop packaging.
18. On an incorrect label, printing error, failed line monitor, or other failed in-process check: segregate product back to the last acceptable check.
19. On an incorrect label, printing error, failed line monitor, or other failed in-process check: notify the Packaging Supervisor and Quality Assurance.
20. Quality Assurance dispositions the affected product, and restart requires the authorization recorded under section 4.6.

### 4.6 Reconcile labeling and packaging materials

1. For every batch, count the printed labeling issued, used, returned, and destroyed. Labels are counted, not trusted.
2. Reconcile the quantities for every batch.
3. Document the reconciliation results for every batch.
4. Stop the line if any discrepancy is found in the labeling count.
5. Do not restart until the discrepancy has been investigated and the authorized restart decision has been recorded. Restart after a labeling-count discrepancy requires Quality Assurance authorization; the investigation and its documentation are completed under SOP-QA-010 before restart.
6. Keep returned labeling identified and stored to prevent mix-ups while completing the batch reconciliation.
7. At completion, destroy all excess labeling bearing batch or control numbers.
8. At completion, record the destruction of all excess labeling bearing batch or control numbers.
9. Uncoded printed materials may be returned to stock under this procedure: identity and count are verified by Packaging with Quality Assurance before return.
10. The return of uncoded printed materials is recorded in FL-EDMS.
11. Reconcile bulk product and printed packaging materials against the number of units produced.
12. Investigate and satisfactorily account for significant or unusual discrepancies before product release. Printed labeling must be 100 percent accounted for; any unexplained count discrepancy is significant. Bulk and packaging material yields are reconciled against the limits in the Packaging Instructions.
13. Quality Assurance decides whether a discrepancy is satisfactorily accounted for before release.
14. Reconciliation results, discrepancies, investigations, and destruction records are recorded in the batch packaging record in FL-EDMS; no additional site-specific reconciliation requirements apply.

### 4.7 Complete and review the batch record

1. Complete a batch packaging record for each batch or part-batch.
2. Record the product name and batch number; packaging dates and times; operator and checker identities for significant steps; identity and conformity checks and in-process results; packaging operations and equipment or line used; and, when available, samples of printed materials, including batch coding, expiry dating, or other overprinting. Operator and checker identities are recorded for label and printing setup, code verification, line clearance, and reconciliation; a checker identity is required for printing and code verification and for reconciliation.
3. Record special problems and unusual events.
4. Document and obtain signed authorization for deviations from the Packaging Instructions.
5. Record the quantities and identities of printed packaging materials and bulk product issued, used, destroyed, or returned to stock, and the quantity of product obtained.
6. Have the person responsible for packaging operations approve the completed batch packaging record. The Packaging Supervisor is the person responsible for packaging operations. The review sequence is operator completion, Label Reviewer check, Packaging Supervisor approval, then Quality Assurance review under SOP-QA-006; the record is held in FL-EDMS.
7. Do not release a batch with an unresolved significant or unusual reconciliation discrepancy. The release decision is made by Quality Assurance under SOP-QA-006, Batch Record Review and Product Release.

## 5. Records

Labeling and packaging records, including material receipt records, issuance and verification records, line clearance checklists, in-process check results, reconciliation and destruction records, and batch packaging records, retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
