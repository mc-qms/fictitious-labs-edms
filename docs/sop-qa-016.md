# SOP-QA-016 — Process Validation

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how manufacturing processes are designed, qualified, and monitored so that they consistently produce product meeting its specifications.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the validation and continued verification of manufacturing processes at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Manufacturing, Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory.

Does not cover cleaning validation, analytical procedure validation, or computer system validation, which have their own procedures (SOP-QA-017, SOP-QC-003, and SOP-QA-018).

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Validation Engineer**
  - Draft validation plans, protocols, and reports.
  - Coordinate execution.
  - Evaluate results against acceptance criteria.
  - Recommend release of each validation stage.

- **Process Owner**
  - Own process knowledge for the process being validated.
  - Propose critical quality attributes, critical process parameters, and acceptance criteria, with their basis.
  - Provide manufacturing participation in validation activities.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Provide quality oversight across the validation lifecycle.
  - Approve validation plans, protocols, and reports.
  - Authorize protocol execution.
  - Release each validation stage.
  - The QA Manager approves the Validation Master Plan and releases the process to commercial manufacture on approval of the final qualification report.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Apply a lifecycle approach

1. Treat process validation as a lifecycle that includes process design, process qualification, and continued process verification. Qualification batches provide part of the evidence for process control; they do not replace process design or continued verification.
2. Use process and product knowledge to support process design, the control strategy, validation, and continued improvement.
3. Reassess risks as knowledge increases or changes occur.
4. Apply quality risk management throughout the lifecycle under SOP-QA-009, Quality Risk Management.

### 4.2 Design the process and plan validation

1. Use development and process knowledge to identify product quality attributes and process parameters that require control.
2. Document the basis for identifying critical quality attributes (CQAs) and critical process parameters (CPPs), including the risk assessment.
3. Define the process control strategy using the available understanding of material attributes, process parameters, equipment, in-process controls, and product specifications.
4. Define the commercial process.
5. Identify relevant differences from development or other manufacturing processes.
6. Assess how those differences affect product quality and process performance.
7. Make the relevant development and process knowledge available to the manufacturing site, unless a documented justification supports another approach.
8. Plan validation activities across the process lifecycle.
9. Document the validation strategy, responsibilities, risk assessments, acceptance-criteria approach, and any required requalification in the applicable validation plan or equivalent.
10. Use trained personnel and approved documentation for validation work.
11. Include manufacturing personnel in process validation activities.
12. Before planning validation activities, identify the applicable Validation Master Plan (PLN-001).
13. Use it to define the qualification and validation system, roles and responsibilities, site qualification and validation status, change and deviation management, acceptance-criteria guidance, document references, and validation strategy, including requalification where applicable.

### 4.3 Establish protocol and readiness

1. Before execution, prepare a protocol or equivalent that defines the validation approach and how results will be evaluated.
2. Quality Assurance approves the protocol.
3. Quality Assurance authorizes its execution.
4. Include the following elements in every protocol; the batch-release element is included whenever validation batches may be released for distribution, as decided by Quality Assurance at protocol approval:
   - A process description and reference to the applicable Master Batch Record.
   - Responsibilities for protocol execution and review.
   - CQAs, CPPs, their limits, and the basis for acceptance criteria.
   - Other monitored attributes or parameters and the reason for monitoring them.
   - Equipment, facilities, monitoring and recording equipment, and calibration status.
   - Analytical methods and their validation status for the intended use.
   - In-process controls, additional tests, and their criteria and rationale.
   - The sampling plan and its rationale.
   - How results will be recorded and evaluated.
   - The batch-release or certification process, if applicable.
5. Before validation batches begin, confirm that critical starting- and packaging-material suppliers are qualified.
6. If this is not done, document the quality-risk-management justification.
7. Confirm that the equipment, facilities, utilities, systems, and test methods needed for the process are qualified or validated for their intended use.
8. Verify qualification status against the FL-EDMS equipment and system records and test-method validation status against SOP-QC-003, Analytical Procedure Validation.
9. Record the check in the protocol's readiness checklist.
10. Predefine the release requirements and process for validation batches. Validation batches are released under SOP-QA-006, Batch Record Review and Product Release, and are not distributed until the qualification report is approved; Quality Assurance is the release authority.
11. Obtain authorization to execute the protocol from Quality Assurance before execution begins.

### 4.4 Qualify supporting equipment and systems

1. Confirm that equipment, facilities, utilities, and systems used in process validation are qualified for their intended use.
2. Where qualification is required, define and document the requirements and acceptance criteria before execution. Base qualification activities on process and equipment knowledge, and include tests of operating limits or worst-case conditions where applicable.
3. Complete installation and operational qualification before performance qualification. Operational qualification normally follows installation qualification. A combined installation/operational qualification may be used when appropriate to the equipment's complexity. Performance qualification normally follows successful installation and operational qualification, but may be performed with operational qualification or process validation where appropriate.
4. A combined or differently sequenced approach is approved by the Validation Engineer and Quality Assurance.
5. The justification for a combined or differently sequenced approach is documented in the protocol.
6. For performance qualification, use production materials, qualified substitutes, or simulated product shown to behave equivalently under normal operating conditions.
7. Include worst-case batch sizes where applicable.
8. Justify sampling frequency for qualification tests.
9. Cover the intended operating range unless development evidence supports a narrower range.
10. Document and execute the applicable qualification pathway under SOP-QA-026, Equipment, Facilities, Utilities, and Systems Qualification.
11. Define requirements and acceptance criteria before execution.
12. Document the results of the qualification stages and their evaluation.

### 4.5 Qualify the manufacturing process

1. Use a traditional, continuous-verification, or hybrid approach.
2. Document the selected approach and its scientific basis.
3. For traditional validation, manufacture batches under routine conditions to demonstrate reproducibility. Validation batches should normally match intended commercial scale.
4. Justify other batch sizes.
5. Determine the number of qualification batches and the sampling plan based on process variability and risk. Do not select batch or sample numbers by convention alone. The justification should provide enough information to assess normal variation and trends. Three consecutive batches under routine conditions are generally acceptable; justify alternatives using relevant standard methods and knowledge of similar products or processes at the site.
6. Validate intended marketed strengths and manufacturing sites.
7. Document the justification for any bracketing of strengths, batch sizes, or pack sizes or container types. For site transfers, consider available product and prior validation knowledge when justifying the batch number.
8. Execute the approved protocol under routine manufacturing conditions.
9. Record results against the predefined acceptance criteria.
10. Document significant protocol changes during execution, including changes to acceptance criteria or operating parameters.
11. Scientifically justify significant protocol changes during execution.
12. Record them as deviations.
13. Route them through SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations.
14. Record results that fail to meet predefined acceptance criteria as deviations.
15. Fully investigate them through SOP-QA-010.
16. Assess any implications for validation before making validation conclusions.
17. Report any implications for validation before making validation conclusions.
18. Summarize the results against acceptance criteria in a validation report. Include the review, conclusions, and recommendation on the validation outcome.
19. Document any scientifically justified change to acceptance criteria.
20. Obtain formal authorization from Quality Assurance before proceeding to the next validation stage. Conditional authorization may be used only when unresolved criteria or deviations have been assessed and documented as having no significant impact on the next activity.
21. The QA Manager releases the process to commercial manufacture on approval of the final qualification report.

### 4.6 Use continuous or hybrid verification approaches

1. Use continuous process verification in place of traditional validation only when product development provides a scientific basis for high assurance from the control strategy.
2. Define a science-based verification strategy that addresses incoming-material attributes, CQAs, and CPPs.
3. Set out how data will be collected, evaluated, and used to assess process performance.
4. Justify the number of batches and amount of data needed to provide assurance of consistent quality.
5. Review the strategy regularly. A hybrid approach may use relevant manufacturing experience and historical batch data, including after changes or during ongoing verification.
6. Update the strategy as process understanding changes.
7. A continuous or hybrid approach is selected only where process understanding and the control strategy support it, on a risk assessment under SOP-QA-009 approved by the QA Manager, recorded in the validation plan.

### 4.7 Monitor the process after qualification

1. Monitor process performance and product quality throughout commercial manufacture.
2. Evaluate trends to confirm that the process remains in a state of control.
3. Use statistical tools where appropriate to assess variability, capability, and continued control.
4. Periodically review the extent and frequency of continued verification.
5. Adjust monitoring based on current process understanding and performance.
6. Use continued-verification results to support validated status in the Product Quality Review under SOP-QA-021, Annual Product Quality Review.
7. Consider incremental changes and whether additional actions, such as enhanced sampling, are needed.
8. Conduct continued verification under an approved protocol or equivalent.
9. Report the results. Results are reviewed at least annually; a significant adverse trend, a major change, or a critical deviation triggers an earlier review.
10. Quality Assurance may change the continued-verification requirements through SOP-QA-004, Change Control.

### 4.8 Manage changes and requalification

1. Assess proposed changes that may affect product quality or process reproducibility for their potential impact on validation and continued verification. Use quality risk management to determine whether additional validation, verification, or requalification is needed.
2. Review supporting data before final change approval.
3. After implementation, evaluate whether the change achieved its objective and whether it affected product quality.
4. Route proposed changes through SOP-QA-004, Change Control, for documented assessment and approval, including the risk-based assessment of section 4.8.1, review of supporting data before final approval, and post-implementation evaluation.
5. Evaluate equipment, facilities, utilities, and systems at an appropriate frequency to confirm they remain in a state of control.
6. When periodic requalification is used, justify the interval.
7. When periodic requalification is used, define the evaluation criteria.
8. Requalification frequency is determined by Quality Assurance with the Validation Engineer, from risk and continued-verification data.
9. Requalification criteria are approved by Quality Assurance.

## 5. Records

Validation records, including the Validation Master Plan, validation plans, protocols and readiness checklists, execution records, deviation records, reports and stage-release approvals, continued-verification protocols and reports, and requalification records, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
