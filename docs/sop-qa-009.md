# SOP-QA-009 — Quality Risk Management

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how risks to product quality are identified, assessed, controlled, communicated, and reviewed across the product lifecycle.

## 2. Scope

Applies to quality risk assessments performed at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory, Manufacturing. Development-stage and technology-transfer risks are in scope.

Does not cover occupational safety risk assessments or business and financial risk management.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Risk Assessment Lead**
  - Facilitate the assessment.
  - Apply the selected method.
  - Document the assessment and its conclusions.
  - Assigned by Quality Assurance for each assessment.

- **Subject Matter Expert**
  - Provide the process, product, and scientific knowledge the assessment needs.
  - Evaluate the risks in their area.
  - Assigned by their function head.

- **Risk Owner**
  - The head of the function accountable for the risk.
  - Select controls for the risks they own.
  - Implement controls for the risks they own.
  - Accept the review schedule for the assessment.
  - Keep the assessment current.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Assign assessment numbers and Risk Assessment Leads.
  - Assess the adequacy of controls.
  - Approve every formal assessment.
  - Accept residual risk (with the QA Manager, where the residual risk is rated high).

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Initiate and identify risks

1. Apply quality risk management to risks identified in advance and to risks assessed after an event. Any function may initiate an assessment by request to Quality Assurance, which assigns the assessment number (format QRM-<year>-<number>, e.g., QRM-2026-001) and the Risk Assessment Lead.
2. Consider risks throughout the product lifecycle, including development, investigational product manufacture, technology transfer, commercial manufacture, and product discontinuation.
3. Document the risk question: the affected product, process, or system; the lifecycle stage; the potential harm and the hazard that could cause it; and the information and data the assessment will use.
4. Identify the risks to be assessed with the Subject Matter Experts.

### 4.2 Assess risks

1. Evaluate each risk using relevant scientific knowledge, available data, and experience with the process. Link the evaluation to patient protection.
2. Make the effort, formality, and documentation proportionate to the level of risk. Do not apply one assessment template or level of detail to every question.
3. Apply the method set in the assessment template maintained in FL-EDMS: risk ranking and filtering for simple questions; failure mode and effects analysis for process risks.
4. Score severity and occurrence — and detectability, where the method uses it — on the template's 1–5 scales.
5. Rate the risk against the template's acceptance criteria.

### 4.3 Control risks

1. Evaluate controls for each identified risk.
2. Select controls with the Risk Owner.
3. Document each control, who implements it, and how its adequacy is assessed.
4. Quality Assurance assesses the adequacy of the selected controls.
5. Base risk decisions on the available science and data.
6. State the residual risk after controls explicitly in the assessment; do not imply it.
7. Quality Assurance approves every formal assessment.
8. Quality Assurance accepts the residual risk. A residual risk rated high under the template's criteria also requires QA Manager approval before the affected activity proceeds.

### 4.4 Communicate risks

1. Communicate the assessment and its conclusions, when the assessment is completed, to the functions affected by the risk or its controls and to Quality Assurance, through the assessment record in FL-EDMS; each communication is documented in that record. Assessment outcomes are also inputs to management review under SOP-QA-008.

### 4.5 Review risks

1. The Risk Owner reviews the assessment when a trigger occurs — a relevant change, a deviation or complaint trend, an incident, or new scientific or process knowledge — and, for product risks, at least at the annual product quality review under SOP-QA-021. Updates are versioned in FL-EDMS and re-approved by Quality Assurance; the review outcome is documented in the assessment record.
2. When an investigation leads to corrective or preventive actions, the CAPA Owner, with Quality Assurance, assesses their effectiveness using quality risk management principles; the assessment is documented in the CAPA record under SOP-QA-011.

## 5. Records

Quality risk assessment records (QRM-<year>-<number>), including the risk question, method and scoring, controls and their adequacy assessments, residual-risk acceptance, communications, and reviews, retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
