# SOP-QC-002 — Stability Testing

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Control

## 1. Purpose

Establish how stability studies are designed, conducted, and evaluated to support shelf life, retest periods, and storage conditions for drug substances and products.

## 2. Scope

Applies to registration, commitment, and ongoing stability studies at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Control laboratory, Quality Assurance. Does not cover stability of clinical trial materials or in-use stability of multi-dose presentations after first opening, which are assessed per product.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Analyst**
  - Record testing results and identify the methods, equipment, and samples used. Testing and calculations are verified by the QC reviewer as second-person verification, documented by the reviewer's initials and date in the record.

- **Stability Coordinator**
  - A designated Quality Control role. Draft study protocols, schedule studies and sample pulls in FL-EDMS, monitor study progress and storage conditions, trend results, and draft study reports and evaluations.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Approve every study protocol and amendment before study work begins, with the approval recorded in FL-EDMS. Approve study reports and proposed retest periods, shelf lives, and storage statements before use; the initial retest period or shelf life for a product also requires QA Manager approval. Determine, with the Stability Coordinator, the impact of deviations and storage-condition excursions on study results.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Plan the study

1. Identify whether the study supports registration, a post-approval commitment, or ongoing stability monitoring.
2. Identify the material, batch, formulation or strength, container-closure system, proposed storage conditions, testing intervals, and attributes to be evaluated in the study protocol.
3. Set sample size and testing intervals using statistical criteria that provide valid estimates of stability.
4. The Stability Coordinator prepares each protocol.
5. Quality Assurance approves it, with the approval recorded in FL-EDMS, and no study work begins before documented approval.
6. Select attributes that may change during storage and affect quality, safety, or efficacy. Consider relevant physical, chemical, biological, and microbiological attributes.
7. Use validated, stability-indicating test methods. For drug-substance registration studies, use stress testing to elucidate degradation pathways and intrinsic stability. Stress testing is performed on one primary batch and includes temperatures in 10 °C increments above accelerated conditions, humidity such as 75% RH or greater where appropriate, oxidation, photolysis, and hydrolysis across a wide pH range in solution or suspension; any product-specific stress-testing approach is documented in the protocol. Specific degradation products need not be examined if they are shown not to form under accelerated or long-term storage conditions.
8. For registration studies on a drug substance, use at least three primary batches at a minimum of pilot scale.
9. Use the intended production synthetic route and manufacturing method.
10. Use the proposed or simulated storage and distribution container-closure system.
11. For registration studies on a drug product, use at least three primary batches of the proposed formulation and container-closure system, made by a process that simulates production.
12. Use at least two pilot-scale batches. A smaller third batch may be used if justified.
13. Study each strength and container size unless scientifically justified bracketing or matrixing is used.
14. Use the proposed marketed packaging for drug-product studies.
15. Perform photostability testing on at least one primary batch when appropriate.
16. Apply bracketing or matrixing only when scientifically justified.
17. For products requiring reconstitution, include testing at dispensing as directed by the labeling and after reconstitution.
18. For constituted or diluted products, test through the proposed in-use period on primary batches at the initial and final time points. If full shelf-life long-term data are unavailable at submission, also test at 12 months or the last available time point. This testing generally does not need to be repeated on commitment batches.
19. For ongoing monitoring, describe the study in a written protocol that extends to the end of the product's shelf life. Include the batches, methods, acceptance criteria, container-closure systems, testing intervals, storage conditions, and other product-specific parameters. A different protocol from the initial long-term study may be used if the difference is justified and documented.
20. Whether a study design or testing approach needs product-specific scientific justification is determined by the Stability Coordinator with Quality Assurance; the justification is documented in the study protocol and approved with it.

### 4.2 Set storage conditions and testing intervals

FL-101 drug substance is stored frozen; its long-term condition is −20 °C ± 5 °C (section 4.2, step 12). The remaining conditions in this section apply where a study protocol designates them.

1. For general storage studies, select long-term storage at either **25 °C ± 2 °C/60% RH ± 5% RH** or **30 °C ± 2 °C/65% RH ± 5% RH**. The selected long-term condition determines whether intermediate testing is required.
2. The Stability Coordinator selects the condition for the study, based on the product's intended storage and the data to be supported.
3. Records the selection and its basis in the study protocol.
4. For general studies, use accelerated storage at **40 °C ± 2 °C/75% RH ± 5% RH** for 6 months, with at least three time points, including the initial and final points. A drug substance has significant change if it fails to meet specification. For a drug product, significant change includes a **5%** change in assay from the initial value, failure to meet potency criteria for biological or immunological methods, a degradation product exceeding its criterion, or failure of appearance, physical-attribute, or functionality criteria.
5. The Stability Coordinator monitors results for approach to significant change.
6. When results may be approaching significant change, the Stability Coordinator, with Quality Assurance approval, increases testing frequency, documented by protocol amendment.
7. If long-term storage is at **25 °C ± 2 °C/60% RH ± 5% RH**, conduct intermediate testing at **30 °C ± 2 °C/65% RH ± 5% RH** when significant change occurs during 6 months of accelerated testing.
8. Collect at least 6 months of intermediate data from a 12-month study at submission. No intermediate condition is needed when **30 °C ± 2 °C/65% RH ± 5% RH** is the long-term condition.
9. When intermediate testing is required, include at least four time points over 12 months, including the initial and final points.
10. For drug substances or products intended for refrigerated storage, use long-term storage at **5 °C ± 3 °C** and accelerated storage at **25 °C ± 2 °C/60% RH ± 5% RH**.
11. Collect at least 12 months of long-term data and 6 months of accelerated data at submission. If significant change occurs between 3 and 6 months of accelerated testing, base the drug-substance retest period or drug-product shelf life on the available real-time long-term data. If significant change occurs within the first 3 months, address the effect of short-term excursions outside labeled storage conditions, such as during shipment or handling. When appropriate, support that assessment with more frequent testing of one batch for less than 3 months. Testing through 6 months is not needed after significant change occurs within the first 3 months.
12. For drug substances or products intended for freezer storage, use long-term storage at **−20 °C ± 5 °C**.
13. Collect at least 12 months of data at submission.
14. When no accelerated condition is used, test one batch at an elevated temperature for an appropriate period to assess short-term excursions.
15. Assess storage below **−20 °C** case by case.
16. For aqueous-based products in semi-permeable containers, assess water loss as well as relevant physical, chemical, biological, and microbiological attributes.
17. Use these storage conditions: long term at **25 °C ± 2 °C/40% RH ± 5% RH** or **30 °C ± 2 °C/35% RH ± 5% RH**; intermediate at **30 °C ± 2 °C/65% RH ± 5% RH**; and accelerated at **40 °C ± 2 °C/NMT 25% RH**. No intermediate condition is needed if **30 °C ± 2 °C/35% RH ± 5% RH** is the long-term condition.
18. When long-term storage is at **25 °C ± 2 °C/40% RH ± 5% RH**, conduct intermediate testing if a significant change other than water loss occurs during 6 months of accelerated testing. Water loss alone does not trigger intermediate testing; provide data demonstrating no significant water loss throughout the proposed shelf life at **25 °C/40% RH**.

   A water loss of **5%** from the initial value after the equivalent of 3 months at **40 °C/NMT 25% RH** is significant. For containers of **1 mL or less** or unit-dose products, a threshold of **5% or more** may be appropriate if justified;
19. Quality Assurance determines and documents whether the alternative threshold is justified and which threshold applies to the study, in the study protocol.
20. For drug products in impermeable containers, use any controlled or ambient humidity condition. For semi-permeable containers, use the humidity conditions specified in 4.2, step 17.
21. Test long-term samples normally every 3 months during year 1, every 6 months during year 2, and annually thereafter.
22. Continue long-term testing through the proposed retest period or shelf life.
23. Use at least three time points over 6 months for accelerated testing.
24. For ongoing studies, include at least one batch per year for each strength and primary packaging type when product is manufactured during that year, unless otherwise justified.
25. Set the batch number and testing frequency to provide enough data for trend analysis.
26. Study samples are assigned to storage locations (chamber and position) in FL-EDMS when placed on study. Storage conditions are monitored continuously by the chamber monitoring system, with alarms, and the monitoring record is retained with the study records.
27. An excursion beyond the defined tolerance is documented.
28. An excursion exceeding tolerance for more than 24 hours is assessed by the Stability Coordinator with Quality Assurance for its effect on study results, and handled under section 4.4 where the results may be affected.

### 4.3 Place and manage study samples

1. Use samples representative of the batch.
2. Identify each sample with the material or product, batch number, sampling date, and source container, as applicable.
3. Store samples under the conditions specified in the protocol.
4. Protect them from adverse storage conditions and mix-ups.
5. Collect and record samples using an approved written sampling procedure. The procedure must specify the sampling method, equipment, amount, any required subdivision, sample-container type and condition, container identification, storage conditions, applicable precautions, and cleaning and storage of sampling equipment.
6. Test samples using the approved method specified in the protocol and the applicable validated, stability-indicating test method.
7. At each time point, samples are withdrawn against the study schedule in FL-EDMS.
8. Before testing, the analyst confirms the sample identity and its storage history (chamber, position, and any recorded excursions) against the chamber log and the FL-EDMS placement record.
9. Documents the confirmation in the study record.

### 4.4 Test samples and document results

1. Test samples using validated, stability-indicating methods and the acceptance criteria specified in the protocol.
2. Record the material or product name, batch number, applicable specification and method references, test results, observations, calculations, testing dates, and the identities of the persons who performed and reviewed the testing.
3. Identify the equipment used.
4. Record enough detail to trace each result to the sample tested. Include relevant raw data, calculations, units, and comparison with acceptance criteria.
5. The Stability Coordinator trends critical quality-attribute results and checks them for consistency.
6. The QC reviewer critically examines calculations as the second-person verification; the check is recorded by the reviewer's initials and date in the study record.
7. When a result fails acceptance criteria or shows an adverse trend, notify Quality Assurance the same business day.
8. Initiate and document an investigation under SOP-QA-012, Out-of-Specification (OOS) Investigation. The affected time point is not retested to obtain a compliant result; further testing follows the investigation.
9. Record storage-condition excursions that may affect results.
10. Assess their effect and report excursions when judged to affect study results.
11. Describe excursions that exceed defined storage tolerances for more than 24 hours.
12. The Stability Coordinator reviews deviations and storage-condition excursions affecting a study.
13. Quality Assurance determines their impact on the study and on any period or statement the study supports, and the review and decision are documented in the study record.

### 4.5 Evaluate results and establish the proposed period and storage statement

1. Evaluate at least three batches to establish a drug-substance retest period or drug-product shelf life. Consider batch variability and relevant physical, chemical, biological, and microbiological results.
2. For quantitative attributes that change over time, determine when the 95% one-sided confidence limit for the mean curve intersects the acceptance criterion. Combine batch data only when justified by tests of the slopes and zero-time intercepts. Otherwise, use the earliest time any batch no longer meets criteria.
3. Assess model fit when statistical analysis is used. Omit formal statistical analysis only when degradation and variability are negligible, and document the justification.
4. Evaluate assay, degradation products, and other appropriate attributes. For drug products, consider mass balance and relevant product performance or degradation characteristics.
5. Use extrapolation beyond observed long-term data only when justified by the degradation mechanism, accelerated results, model fit, batch size, and supporting data.
6. Base the proposed retest period or shelf life on the stability evaluation. A drug-substance retest period is the time it is expected to meet specification under defined storage conditions. A drug-product shelf life is the period it is expected to meet its approved shelf-life specification under labeled storage conditions.
7. Base storage statements on the stability evaluation and applicable labeling requirements. State specific storage instructions where needed. Do not use "ambient conditions" or "room temperature." Give specific instructions for products that cannot tolerate freezing.
8. For drug products, use stability data to support the expiration date and storage conditions stated in labeling.
9. Proposed retest periods, shelf lives, and storage statements are reviewed and approved by Quality Assurance, with the approval recorded in FL-EDMS, before use; the initial retest period or shelf life for a product also requires QA Manager approval.

### 4.6 Manage commitment and ongoing studies

1. If primary-batch long-term data do not cover the approved shelf life or proposed retest period, continue stability testing after approval using production batches.
2. When three production batches have long-term data covering the approved period, no additional commitment study is needed. Otherwise:
   - If data are available for three production batches, continue those batches through the proposed period.
   - If data are available for fewer than three production batches, continue those batches and add batches to reach at least three.
   - If no production-batch data are available, place the first three production batches on long-term studies through the proposed period.
3. Use the primary-batch protocol for commitment batches unless a different approach is scientifically justified.
4. Apply the relevant accelerated or intermediate testing requirements.
5. Conduct an ongoing stability study after a significant process or packaging change or significant deviation.
6. Quality Assurance, with the Stability Coordinator, decides whether additional batches — including batches from reworking, reprocessing, or recovery operations — are placed on study, and records the decision and its basis in FL-EDMS.
7. Formalize ongoing study results in a report.
8. Make them available to relevant personnel for review.
9. The Stability Coordinator reviews commitment-study results and ongoing trends and escalates adverse trends to Quality Assurance.
10. Quality Assurance, with the Stability Coordinator, assesses at least annually — as an input to SOP-QA-021, Annual Product Quality Review — whether the approved shelf life and storage statement remain suitable.
11. Documents the assessment.

## 5. Records

Stability records, including approved protocols and amendments, sample placement and chamber monitoring records, pull and testing records, trend analyses, excursion assessments, study reports and evaluations, and period and storage-statement approvals, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
