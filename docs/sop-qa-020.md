# SOP-QA-020 — Calibration and Preventive Maintenance

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Engineering and Maintenance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how equipment and instruments are calibrated, maintained, and qualified, so that they remain fit for their intended use and their status is known at all times.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the calibration, preventive maintenance, and qualification of equipment and instruments used in regulated activities at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Engineering and maintenance, Quality Control laboratory, Quality Assurance.

Does not cover corrective repair work orders, which follow the maintenance work-order process in FL-EDMS, or facility systems with no product contact or product impact.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Calibration Technician**
  - Perform calibrations by the approved methods at the scheduled intervals.
  - Record as-found and as-left results.
  - Report an out-of-tolerance result immediately to the Equipment Owner and Quality Assurance.

- **Maintenance Technician**
  - Perform preventive maintenance tasks under each equipment's maintenance plan.
  - Record completion in FL-EDMS.
  - Identify defects found during maintenance.

- **Equipment Owner**
  - The head of the function that owns the equipment. Accountable for the equipment's status, scheduling of calibration and maintenance, impact assessments for out-of-tolerance results, and return-to-use requests.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Provide quality oversight over this procedure.
  - Approve the calibration program and the intervals for GMP-critical instruments.
  - Review out-of-tolerance impact assessments and own the impact decision.
  - Concur in return-to-use decisions for GMP-critical equipment.
  - Authorize qualification stage transitions.

Personnel performing qualification activities must be suitably trained and follow approved procedures. Qualification activities must have appropriate quality oversight over their lifecycle.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Equipment status and control

1. Keep equipment fit for its intended purpose. Design, location, and maintenance must not create a hazard to product quality.
2. Identify equipment status in the equipment register in FL-EDMS — equipment ID, owner, and calibration and maintenance status and due dates — and by a status label on the equipment.
3. The Equipment Owner keeps the register entry current.
4. A status change is entered the day it occurs.
5. If equipment is defective, remove it from production and quality control areas when possible.
6. If it cannot be removed, clearly label it as defective.
7. If it cannot be removed, tag it out in FL-EDMS.
8. Defective equipment returns to use only after repair and any needed recalibration or requalification, on an assessment by the Equipment Owner that the defect and the repair have not affected the equipment's fitness, authorized by the Equipment Owner with Quality Assurance concurrence for GMP-critical equipment.

### 4.2 Calibration

1. Calibrate and check measuring, weighing, recording, and control equipment at defined intervals using appropriate methods.
2. Keep adequate records of calibration and test results.
3. Every measuring, weighing, recording, or control instrument used in a regulated activity is entered in the equipment register with a calibration interval set from the manufacturer's recommendation and the instrument's criticality.
4. Quality Assurance approves the intervals for GMP-critical instruments.
5. Calibrate by the manufacturer's method or another approved method, against standards traceable to national standards. Acceptance is the manufacturer's tolerance or the process requirement, whichever is tighter.
6. Document a failed or out-of-tolerance result with its as-found values.
7. The Equipment Owner, with Quality Assurance, assesses the impact on results produced since the last acceptable calibration; where product impact is possible, the result is handled under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations. Quality Assurance owns the impact decision. The instrument is not returned to GMP use until it is recalibrated acceptably and the impact assessment is complete.

### 4.3 Preventive maintenance

1. Perform preventive maintenance to keep equipment fit for its intended use. Maintenance work must not create a hazard to product quality.
2. Preventive maintenance tasks and frequencies are defined in each equipment's maintenance plan in FL-EDMS, from the manufacturer's recommendations and the equipment's criticality.
3. Completion is recorded in FL-EDMS with the tasks performed and any defects found.
4. Equipment returns to use when the planned tasks are complete and the equipment is verified operational by the Maintenance Technician; equipment with overdue preventive maintenance is out of service for GMP use until the work is complete.
5. Route corrective repair work orders through the maintenance work-order process in FL-EDMS. Corrective repair work orders are outside this procedure.

### 4.4 Qualification planning and execution

1. Plan qualification across the equipment lifecycle, from initial requirements through the end of use. Use quality risk management under SOP-QA-009, Quality Risk Management, to support qualification.
2. Document how risk assessments inform qualification activities.
3. Whether changed knowledge requires a repeat risk assessment is determined by the Equipment Owner with the Validation Engineer and documented in the equipment record in FL-EDMS.
4. Before planning qualification, identify and use the applicable Validation Master Plan (PLN-001). Do not proceed with qualification planning until it is available.
5. Use it to identify the qualification system, roles and responsibilities, equipment and qualification status, change and deviation management, acceptance-criteria guidance, applicable documents, and qualification strategy, including requalification where applicable.
6. Prepare protocols that identify critical systems, attributes, and parameters, with associated acceptance criteria, using the protocol and report formats in PLN-001.
7. Approve and authorize qualification documents as in SOP-QA-016, Process Validation: the Validation Engineer and the Equipment Owner approve the protocol, and Quality Assurance authorizes it.
8. Use the qualification stages determined for the equipment and its qualification plan.
9. Which stages apply is determined by the Validation Engineer with the Equipment Owner against the criteria in PLN-001, and the determination is documented in the qualification plan.

   1. **User requirements and design qualification**
      - Define equipment requirements in a user requirements specification (URS) and/or functional specification. Include essential quality requirements and address GMP risks.
      - Verify that the design meets the requirements.
      - Document that the design meets the requirements.
   2. **Factory or site acceptance testing**
      - For novel or complex equipment, evaluate the equipment at the vendor before delivery when applicable.
      - Before installation, confirm at the vendor site, when applicable, that the equipment meets the URS or functional specification.
      - Where justified, complete documentation review or testing during factory acceptance testing (FAT) without repeating it at installation or operational qualification (IQ/OQ) only when transport and installation do not affect the tested functionality. Site acceptance testing (SAT) may follow receipt when appropriate.
   3. **Installation qualification**
      - Verify installation of components, instrumentation, equipment, pipework, and services against engineering drawings and specifications.
      - Verify installation against predefined criteria.
      - Collect supplier operating and working instructions and maintenance requirements.
      - Calibrate instrumentation.
      - Verify materials of construction.
   4. **Operational qualification**
      - Perform operational qualification (OQ) after IQ, or combine IQ and OQ as an installation/operation qualification (IOQ) when appropriate for equipment complexity.
      - Test that the equipment operates as designed, including upper and lower operating limits and worst-case conditions.
      - Following successful OQ, finalize applicable operating and cleaning procedures, operator training, and preventive maintenance requirements.
   5. **Performance qualification**
      - Normally perform performance qualification (PQ) after successful IQ and OQ. PQ may be performed with OQ or process validation when appropriate.
      - Use production materials, qualified substitutes, or simulated product shown to behave equivalently under normal operating conditions and worst-case batch sizes.
      - Justify sampling frequency used to confirm process control.
      - Cover the intended process operating range unless documented development evidence confirms the operating ranges.
10. Before proceeding to the next qualification stage, obtain formal authorization from Quality Assurance. Conditional authorization may be given when some acceptance criteria or deviations remain unresolved only if a documented assessment — by the Validation Engineer, accepted by Quality Assurance — finds no significant impact on the next activity.
11. The assessment and authorization are documented in the qualification report.
12. Record results that fail predefined acceptance criteria as deviations under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations, following the qualification pathway of SOP-QA-026, Equipment, Facilities, Utilities, and Systems Qualification.
13. Investigate results that fail predefined acceptance criteria fully under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations, following the qualification pathway of SOP-QA-026, Equipment, Facilities, Utilities, and Systems Qualification. Discuss any implications for qualification in the report.
14. Summarize results against acceptance criteria, and document the review and conclusions.
15. Make a final recommendation on the qualification outcome.

### 4.5 Requalification and changes

1. Evaluate equipment at an appropriate frequency to confirm that it remains in a state of control. If requalification is performed at a set interval, justify the interval, define evaluation criteria, and assess the possibility of changes over time.
2. Requalification needs, frequencies, and criteria are determined as in SOP-QA-016, Process Validation — by Quality Assurance with the Validation Engineer, from risk and performance data — and approved by Quality Assurance.
3. Before implementing a planned change that may affect equipment, calibration, maintenance, validation, or product quality, route the change through SOP-QA-004, Change Control.
4. Use quality risk management to assess potential impacts and determine any needed verification, validation, or requalification.
5. Authorize and approve changes through the applicable pharmaceutical quality system.
6. Review supporting data before final approval.
7. After implementation, evaluate effectiveness when appropriate.

## 5. Records

Calibration, maintenance, and qualification records, including the equipment register, calibration certificates and as-found/as-left data, out-of-tolerance impact assessments, maintenance plans and completion records, work orders, qualification protocols and reports, and stage authorizations, retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
