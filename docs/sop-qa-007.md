# SOP-QA-007 — Internal Audit

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how the organization's compliance with its own procedures and with GMP is audited internally, and how findings are recorded, answered, and closed.

## 2. Scope

Applies to planned and for-cause internal audits of every function at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States. Quality Assurance administers the audit program; every function is an auditee under it.

Does not cover audits of suppliers and contractors, which follow SOP-QA-015, Supplier Quality, or regulatory inspections by authorities.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Lead Auditor**
  - Plan the audit.
  - Conduct the audit.
  - Lead the audit team.
  - Communicate findings to the Auditee.
  - Issue the audit report.
  - Verify completion and effectiveness of actions for the audit's findings.

- **Auditor**
  - Prepare for the assigned audit areas.
  - Collect and document objective evidence.
  - Support the Lead Auditor in evaluating findings.

- **Auditee**
  - Provide access to personnel, documents, records, and areas during the audit.
  - Confirm the factual accuracy of findings.
  - Respond to findings within the required time with investigation, actions, and dates.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Own the audit program: maintain the audit schedule, designate auditors, review findings and responses, accept responses, and close findings. The QA Manager approves the annual schedule, authorizes for-cause audits, and receives escalations.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Plan audits

1. Conduct internal audits regularly to assess the effectiveness and applicability of the pharmaceutical quality system.
2. Quality Assurance maintains an annual audit schedule in FL-EDMS (audit numbers in the format AUD-<year>-<number>, e.g., AUD-2026-002), approved by the QA Manager.
3. Set the schedule on a risk basis, using prior audit findings, deviation and corrective and preventive action trends, significant changes, and inspection history as inputs. Every function is audited at least once every 2 years; higher-risk areas are audited annually.
4. Conduct a for-cause audit when triggered by a significant deviation or complaint trend, a suspected data-integrity issue, a major change, or a regulatory inspection finding. The QA Manager authorizes a for-cause audit before it begins.
5. Define the audit's scope, objectives, and criteria before it begins. Criteria are the current effective procedures in FL-EDMS for the area audited and the applicable US requirements (21 CFR Parts 210/211, 600/610, and Part 11).

### 4.2 Prepare and conduct audits

1. Quality Assurance designates a Lead Auditor and, as needed, Auditors for each audit. Auditors are trained under SOP-QA-005, Training and Qualification, and are independent of the area audited; no one audits their own work or an area they are responsible for.
2. Assess whether the applicable processes and procedures are effective and applicable.
3. Conduct the audit through interviews, review of documents and records, observation of activities, and sampling of records proportionate to the audit scope.
4. Document the objective evidence supporting each potential finding.
5. The Lead Auditor communicates potential findings to the Auditee as they arise and at the closing meeting. Factual disagreements are resolved against the objective evidence before the report is issued; unresolved disagreements are recorded in the report.

### 4.3 Record and report findings

1. Record audit results and findings in FL-EDMS, the system of record for the audit program, for consideration in the pharmaceutical quality system, including its corrective and preventive action system under SOP-QA-011 and management review under SOP-QA-008.
2. The Lead Auditor issues the audit report within 10 business days of the closing meeting, to the Auditee's management and the QA Manager. The report records the scope, objectives, and criteria; the evidence reviewed; each finding with its classification — Critical (direct impact on product quality, patient safety, or data integrity), Major (potential impact, no immediate risk), or Minor (no product impact) — and the required response date.
3. The Lead Auditor, with Quality Assurance, assesses each finding for quality risk.
4. The assessment is recorded with the finding.

### 4.4 Obtain and assess responses

1. Quality Assurance decides whether a finding requires investigation or a corrective and preventive action, applying the criteria of SOP-QA-011, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA): a structured investigation aimed at root cause, with effort, formality, and documentation in proportion to the level of risk.
2. Quality Assurance accepts the Auditee's response.
3. The Auditee responds within 15 business days of the report. The response records, for each finding, the investigation performed or planned, the corrective and preventive actions proposed, and a completion date for each action.
4. Responses or actions overdue by 5 business days are escalated by Quality Assurance to the QA Manager; items that remain overdue are carried into management review under SOP-QA-008.

### 4.5 Verify actions and close findings

1. The Lead Auditor verifies completion of each action from objective evidence.
2. The Lead Auditor evaluates the effectiveness of corrective and preventive actions at the check defined for the action.
3. A finding is closed by Quality Assurance when the actions are complete, the verification evidence is recorded, and any required effectiveness check has passed.
4. An ineffective action reopens the finding and is escalated to the QA Manager. A finding that recurs is flagged as a repeat finding in FL-EDMS and trended; findings that remain open are carried into management review under SOP-QA-008.

### 4.6 Review audit results

Include audit results and findings among the inputs to management review under SOP-QA-008, Management Review. Use audits as an input when monitoring the effectiveness of pharmaceutical quality system processes.

## 5. Records

Audit records, including schedules, audit plans, reports, findings and their risk assessments, responses, verification evidence, and closure records (AUD-<year>-<number>), retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
