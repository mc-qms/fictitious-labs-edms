# SOP-QA-010 — Handling of Deviations

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how departures from approved procedures, specifications, and established conditions are recorded, investigated, assessed for impact, and dispositioned.

## 2. Scope

Applies to every departure from an approved procedure, specification, or established condition at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory, Manufacturing.

Does not cover laboratory results that fail specification, which follow SOP-QA-012, Out-of-Specification (OOS) Investigation, or customer complaints, which follow SOP-QA-013, Complaint Handling.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Initiator**
  - Record the deviation when it is found. State what happened, when it happened, and what was affected before any investigation conclusion is drawn.

- **Investigator**
  - Investigate the deviation and assess its potential impact. Set the investigation depth in proportion to the potential impact on product quality and patient safety.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Make the disposition decision for affected product. Quality Assurance, as part of the Quality Unit, is authorized to make the disposition decision for affected product.
  - Assign the investigator.
  - Review and approve the investigation record.
  - Approve corrective or preventive actions.
  - Close the deviation.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Record the deviation

Record the deviation when it is found; do not reconstruct the initial record later. Before drawing an investigation conclusion, document what happened, when it happened, and what was affected.

Anyone who discovers a deviation reports it to their supervisor and to Quality Assurance the same business day. Quality Assurance initiates a deviation record in FL-EDMS within 1 business day of discovery and assigns the deviation number (format DEV-<year>-<number>, e.g., DEV-2026-014). The record states the description of the event, the date and time it was discovered, who discovered it, the lots, materials, and equipment affected, and any immediate actions taken.

### 4.2 Investigate the deviation

Investigate the deviation to determine its cause and identify appropriate corrective or preventive action. Set the depth of investigation in proportion to the potential impact on product quality and patient safety.

Use relevant product and process monitoring information in the investigation. Apply an appropriate level of root cause analysis. Before attributing a deviation to human error, consider whether process, procedural, or system-based issues were overlooked. If the true root cause cannot be determined, consider the most likely root cause or causes.

Quality Assurance assigns an investigator from the function that owns the affected activity, and the investigator works together with Quality Assurance. The investigation is completed within 30 calendar days of initiation; one extension of up to 30 additional days may be granted by the QA Manager with documented justification. Quality Assurance reviews and approves the completed investigation record.

### 4.3 Assess impact and risk

Assess potential impacts on product quality and patient safety using scientific knowledge and available process experience. Identify relevant hazards and consider the likelihood and severity of potential harm. Document assumptions, uncertainty, and gaps in available information that affect the assessment.

Use documented, transparent, and reproducible methods. Match the formality and documentation of the assessment to the risk, uncertainty, and complexity of the issue. Determine whether controls or further assessment are needed. Reassess risk when new information or experience could affect the decision.

Quality Assurance classifies each deviation using the impact assessment: **Critical** — direct impact on product quality, patient safety, or data integrity; **Major** — potential impact, with no immediate risk; **Minor** — no product impact. A Critical classification is an escalation trigger: the QA Manager is notified the same business day. Affected product is held under Section 4.4 until disposition.

### 4.4 Disposition affected product

Quality Assurance makes the disposition decision for affected product.

While disposition is pending, affected lots and materials are placed on QA hold — identified as held and not used, shipped, or released. No affected lot is released before the deviation is closed or Quality Assurance documents an interim disposition. Disposition options are: release as-is, with documented justification; rework or reprocess under an approved procedure; or reject and destroy. The disposition, its justification, and the Quality Assurance signature are documented in the deviation record.

### 4.5 Corrective and preventive action and follow-up

Identify and take appropriate corrective or preventive action based on the investigation. Monitor and assess the effectiveness of actions taken. Reassess risk when action implementation or new information could affect the assessment.

Every deviation is assessed for CAPA under SOP-QA-011, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA); Critical and Major deviations require a documented CAPA determination. Quality Assurance approves corrective or preventive actions; where a CAPA is opened, the actions and their effectiveness checks are managed under SOP-QA-011. Quality Assurance closes the deviation when the investigation is complete and approved, the disposition has been executed, and the required actions are complete or transferred to a CAPA record.

## 5. Records

Deviation records (DEV-<year>-<number>), including the initial report, investigation, impact assessment, disposition, and closure, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
