# SOP-QA-012 — Out-of-Specification (OOS) Investigation

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how out-of-specification and out-of-trend laboratory results are investigated, so that a result is invalidated only for a documented, scientifically sound cause and valid results are acted on.

## 2. Scope

Applies to every out-of-specification and out-of-trend result generated by the quality control laboratory at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory, Manufacturing.

Does not cover out-of-specification results on stability studies' alert limits before a specification failure, which follow SOP-QC-002, Stability Testing, or microbiological excursions in environmental monitoring, which follow SOP-QC-001, Environmental Monitoring.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Analyst**
  - Notify the Laboratory Supervisor when an out-of-specification or out-of-trend result is identified.
  - Preserve the original data and records.
  - Provide information needed for review.

- **Laboratory Supervisor**
  - Coordinate the initial laboratory review, ensure the Analyst's work is examined before a full-scale batch investigation is opened.
  - Document the review and its findings.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Independently review the investigation and ensure its conclusions and follow-up are documented. Quality Assurance, as part of the Quality Unit, holds the authority under this procedure: only Quality Assurance may invalidate a result, and only for a documented, scientifically sound laboratory cause; Quality Assurance decides the disposition of affected batches; and Quality Assurance escalates to the QA Manager when a result is confirmed out of specification or an affected lot has been distributed.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Identify and control the result

1. Identify the approved written specification that applies to the result before comparing the result with the specification. The applicable specification is the approved FL-101 drug substance specification cited in the test method for the result.
2. Compare the result with the established trend limits. The trend limits and out-of-trend evaluation criteria for each test are established and maintained by Quality Assurance.
3. Do not average, retest, or resample a result for the purpose of obtaining a compliant result. A result stands unless the investigation assigns a documented, scientifically sound laboratory cause for invalidating it.
4. The Analyst notifies the Laboratory Supervisor and Quality Assurance the same business day an out-of-specification or out-of-trend result is identified.
5. Quality Assurance assigns the investigation number (format OOS-<year>-<number>, e.g., OOS-2026-002).
6. Quality Assurance notifies the QA Manager within 1 business day when a result is confirmed out of specification or the affected lot has been distributed.
7. Keep a batch in quarantine until Quality Assurance releases it.
8. If the batch is already distributed or otherwise outside quarantine, Quality Assurance notifies the QA Manager immediately.
9. The QA Manager escalates.
10. Quality Assurance assesses the distributed product, and a field action or recall is assessed under SOP-QA-014, Product Recall.

### 4.2 Complete the laboratory phase first

1. The Laboratory Supervisor must ensure the Analyst's work is examined before opening any full-scale investigation of the batch. Complete the laboratory phase first and within a defined time.
2. The laboratory phase is completed within 5 business days of identification of the result. If that limit cannot be met, the Laboratory Supervisor documents the reason in the investigation record and Quality Assurance may grant one extension of up to 5 additional business days.
3. Review the original laboratory records for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with established standards. Examine the sample description and source, the test method, the data and calculations, the result compared with the applicable specification, and the second-person review.
4. The laboratory investigation checks, as applicable: the Analyst's technique and adherence to the test method; the calculations and transcriptions; instrument performance and calibration status; the preparation, identity, and expiry of standards and reagents; sample preparation, including dilutions; and system suitability or other method controls. A laboratory cause may be assigned only when documented objective evidence shows the error occurred.
5. Quality Assurance determines whether the assigned cause is sufficient to invalidate the result.
6. Document the laboratory review, the evidence considered, and its conclusion. Do not invalidate the result unless the investigation documents a scientifically sound laboratory cause.

### 4.3 Determine whether to expand the investigation

1. For an out-of-specification result, if the laboratory phase does not assign a documented, scientifically sound laboratory cause, retain the result as valid.
2. Open a full-scale investigation of the batch.
3. Investigate whether the failure or discrepancy may affect associated batches or products.
4. Document the investigation, its conclusions, and follow-up.
5. Quality Assurance opens and leads the full-scale investigation, together with the Quality Control laboratory and Manufacturing.
6. The investigation reviews the batch record and associated records, identifies potentially affected batches and products — including batches sharing materials, equipment, or a manufacturing campaign — and places affected batches on QA hold pending disposition.
7. The full-scale investigation is completed within 30 calendar days of opening.
8. A CAPA determination is made under SOP-QA-011, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA).
9. For an out-of-trend result, Quality Assurance owns the evaluation, together with the Quality Control laboratory. A result outside its established trend limits but within specification triggers a documented evaluation of the trend, the method, and the process. A full-scale batch investigation is required only when the evaluation identifies a potential impact on product quality; otherwise the evaluation is closed with Quality Assurance approval and the result is trended.

### 4.4 Conclude and act on the investigation

1. Document whether the original result remains valid or is invalidated. If it is invalidated, document the scientifically sound laboratory cause supporting that conclusion.
2. For a valid out-of-specification result showing that a drug product fails established standards or specifications, reject the product. Reprocessing may be performed; before acceptance and use, reprocessed material must meet applicable standards, specifications, and other relevant criteria. Quality Assurance makes these decisions as part of the Quality Unit; rejection of a lot, and any disposition of distributed product, requires QA Manager approval.
3. For a valid out-of-trend result, the lot is not rejected on that basis alone: Quality Assurance documents the evaluation and its impact assessment, and determines the follow-up, which may include enhanced trending, an investigation under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations, or a CAPA determination under SOP-QA-011.
4. Document investigation conclusions and follow-up.
5. The Laboratory Supervisor reviews the laboratory phase.
6. Quality Assurance reviews the complete investigation and closes it, and the full-scale investigation closes within 30 calendar days of opening.
7. Follow-up actions are tracked in FL-EDMS in the investigation record or, where opened, in the related deviation or CAPA record.

## 5. Records

OOS investigation records (OOS-<year>-<number>), including the laboratory review, the full-scale investigation where opened, result validity and disposition decisions, and closure, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
