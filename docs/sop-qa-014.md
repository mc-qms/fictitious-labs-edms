# SOP-QA-014 — Product Recall

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how a decision to recall product from the market is made, executed, reconciled, and reported, and how recall readiness is maintained and tested.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the recall of any distributed product of Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Regulatory Affairs, Warehouse and distribution.

Does not cover market withdrawals of product for purely commercial reasons with no quality defect.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **QA Manager**
  - Decide whether to initiate a recall, based on the documented risk assessment. The decision is made on risk to patients, not commercial cost, and may not be overruled for commercial reasons.
  - Approve the recall strategy's effectiveness-check level and any public warning.
  - Determine that recall activities are complete.
  - Approve closure.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Assess the quality defect and patient risk.
  - Oversee recall activities.
  - Decide whether retention samples are checked or tested in support of the assessment.
  - Approve the content of recall communications and the disposition of returned product.

- **Recall Coordinator**
  - A member of Quality Assurance designated by the QA Manager for each recall event.
  - Coordinate recall activities.
  - Maintain the recall record.
  - Coordinate distribution tracing and communications.
  - Track responses and returned product.
  - Prepare status updates and the final reconciliation report.

- **Regulatory Affairs**
  - Coordinate communications with the FDA, including timely notification and submission of recall information and status updates.
  - Assess biological product deviation reporting under 21 CFR 600.14.

- **Executive Management**
  - Provide the people and resources needed to assess and conduct a recall. Do not delay or prevent a Quality Assurance recall decision for commercial reasons.

- **Warehouse and Distribution**
  - Receive returned product against the recall record.
  - Identify and segregate it.
  - Maintain distribution records that permit prompt tracing by lot.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Identify and assess a potential recall

1. Anyone who becomes aware of a potential recall event — a quality defect in distributed product, a complaint trend, a confirmed out-of-specification result on a distributed lot, or a regulatory action affecting distributed product — notifies Quality Assurance the same business day.
2. Quality Assurance logs the report in FL-EDMS.
3. Quality Assurance informs the QA Manager within 1 business day; a report indicating an imminent risk to patients is escalated to the QA Manager immediately.
4. Document the reported defect or suspected defect, including the original details.
5. Assess the report's validity and the extent of the potential defect.
6. Identify the affected product and batch or batches.
7. Review relevant production, certification, and distribution records.
8. Quality Assurance decides whether reference or retention samples are checked or tested, based on whether the examination can confirm the defect, bound the affected population, or inform the risk assessment.
9. The decision and its basis are documented in the recall record.
10. Assess whether other batches or products may be affected, including batches that may contain portions of the affected batch or defective components.
11. Review prior defect reports for recurring problems.
12. Assess the risk to patients. Consider the severity and extent of the defect, whether injuries or illness have occurred, the likelihood and consequences of harm, and the populations that may be at greatest risk.
13. Document the assessment and its basis.
14. Assess whether the defect may involve falsification.

### 4.2 Decide whether to initiate a recall

1. Before deciding on a recall or other risk-reducing action, Regulatory Affairs, with Quality Assurance, assesses whether the action could affect product availability to patients, including the risk of a shortage where the product has no authorized alternative.
2. The QA Manager approves the assessment.
3. If a shortage risk is identified, Regulatory Affairs notifies the FDA promptly.
4. The assessment and any notification are documented in the recall record.
5. The QA Manager decides whether to initiate a recall based on the risk to patients. The decision is not based on commercial cost, and a recall may be initiated without commercial approval.
6. Make the decision promptly. Do not wait for the root cause or full extent of the defect to be established when an earlier risk-reducing action is needed to protect patients.
7. Record the decision in the recall record in FL-EDMS (number format REC-<year>-<number>, e.g., REC-2026-001, assigned by Quality Assurance): the defect and affected lots, the risk assessment, the decision and its rationale, the decision-maker, the Recall Coordinator and Regulatory Affairs as participants, and the date.
8. If a recall is not initiated, document the basis for that decision in the same record.

### 4.3 Plan and notify

1. The Recall Coordinator coordinates a recall strategy for the specific event.
2. Document:
   - The product and batches involved and the reason for the recall.
   - The depth of the recall: user or consumer, retail, or wholesale level, as appropriate to the event.
   - Whether a public warning is needed and, if so, its intended audience and distribution.
   - The method and level of effectiveness checks.
   - Instructions for affected accounts on stopping further distribution or use and handling affected product.
3. Set the recall depth and communications based on the risk, distribution, how readily the product and defect can be identified, how much product may remain unused, and the continued availability of essential products.
4. The Recall Coordinator proposes an effectiveness-check level for the recall strategy: Level A, 100 percent of consignees; Level B, a case-specific percentage greater than 10 percent and less than 100 percent; Level C, 10 percent; Level D, 2 percent; or Level E, no checks. The selection is based on the risk assessment, the recall depth, and the number of consignees; a Level B percentage is set case by case, proportionate to the risk, and documented in the strategy.
5. The QA Manager approves the effectiveness-check level.
6. Specify the method or methods to contact consignees.
7. Use current distribution records to identify affected consignees, including their addresses, phone and/or fax numbers usable inside and outside working hours, batches, and quantities delivered.
8. Make these records readily available to the people responsible for the recall.
9. Regulatory Affairs notifies the FDA immediately when the organization initiates removal or correction because it believes a distributed product may be violative.
10. Regulatory Affairs assesses whether a biological product deviation report is required under 21 CFR 600.14. FDA is the regulatory authority for Fictitious Labs' products in the United States.
11. Regulatory Affairs also determines whether any state authority must be notified, based on the distribution of the affected product and applicable state requirements.
12. The Recall Coordinator drafts concise recall communications that identify the product and affected batches, explain the reason and hazard, state what recipients must do, and provide a way to report whether they have affected product.
13. Quality Assurance approves the content.
14. A public warning is also approved by the QA Manager.
15. Communications are issued promptly to affected direct accounts by the methods specified in the recall strategy.
16. Each issuance is documented in the recall record.

### 4.4 Execute and monitor the recall

1. Issue the recall communications according to the recall strategy.
2. Direct affected accounts to stop further distribution or use and to follow the stated product-handling instructions.
3. Track which consignees were notified, how and when they were notified, their responses, and the quantities of product they report having on hand.
4. Follow up with nonresponding consignees according to the strategy.
5. Conduct effectiveness checks when the strategy specifies Levels A through D. For Level E, do not conduct effectiveness checks.
6. Document effectiveness checks when the strategy specifies Levels A through D.
7. Verify that consignees at the selected recall depth received the notification and took the required action.
8. Provide recall status reports to the FDA at the frequency specified for the recall. Include notification and response counts, product returned or corrected, quantities accounted for, effectiveness-check results, and the estimated time to completion.
9. Warehouse receives returned product against the recall record.
10. Warehouse verifies its identity and quantity.
11. Warehouse logs it in FL-EDMS.
12. Returned product is identified with recall status labeling.
13. Returned product is stored separately in the designated secure quarantine area while its disposition is pending.
14. Only Quality Assurance dispositions recalled product.
15. The formal disposition of each recalled batch is documented in the recall record.

### 4.5 Reconcile and close

1. Reconcile quantities distributed, returned, and otherwise accounted for. The quantities must agree.
2. Document the reconciliation and any supporting records.
3. If the quantities do not agree, the Recall Coordinator investigates the mismatch with Warehouse and Quality Assurance.
4. If the quantities do not agree, the Recall Coordinator escalates the mismatch to the QA Manager.
5. The investigation and its documented outcome are recorded in the recall record. Do not proceed to the final report or closure until the discrepancy is resolved and the reconciliation agrees.
6. The Recall Coordinator prepares a final recall report documenting the recall's progress through closure, the reconciliation, effectiveness-check results, and product disposition.
7. Quality Assurance reviews the report.
8. The QA Manager determines that the organization's recall activities are complete.
9. The QA Manager approves closure.
10. Continue to provide recall status reports until the FDA terminates the recall, when applicable.

### 4.6 Maintain and test recall readiness

1. Maintain this written recall procedure under SOP-QA-003, Document Control.
2. Review and update this written recall procedure when necessary, under SOP-QA-003, Document Control.
3. Maintain distribution records that permit prompt identification and location of distributed product by batch.
4. Test recall readiness with a mock recall at least annually, including a traceability exercise from distribution records, covering events during and outside office hours.
5. Quality Assurance reviews the results.
6. Gaps are handled under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations, or SOP-QA-011, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA), as applicable.
7. The evaluation and its rationale are documented in FL-EDMS.

## 5. Records

Recall records (REC-<year>-<number>), including the potential-recall report and assessment, the decision record, the recall strategy, communications and issuance records, consignee tracking and effectiveness-check records, returned-product receipt and disposition records, status reports, the reconciliation, and the final report; mock recall records; retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
