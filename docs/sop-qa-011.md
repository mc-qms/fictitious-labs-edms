# SOP-QA-011 — Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how the causes of nonconformities and quality problems are identified and eliminated, and how the effectiveness of the actions taken is verified.

## 2. Scope

Applies to corrective and preventive actions arising from deviations, complaints, audit findings, and quality trends at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory, Manufacturing.

Does not cover the investigations that feed it, which follow SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations, and SOP-QA-013, Complaint Handling.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Initiator**
  - Provide information about the quality problem and its source, including the investigation conclusions and supporting information, to the CAPA Owner.

- **CAPA Owner**
  - Assess the reported problem and investigation conclusions.
  - Develop the action plan.
  - Carry out the action plan.
  - Define the effectiveness check when planning actions.
  - Document implementation and results.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Oversee the CAPA system.
  - Review CAPA records.
  - Monitor CAPA status and effectiveness.
  - Quality Assurance reviews and approves each CAPA opening and each action plan, and approves closure; the QA Manager approves CAPA closure.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Identify and initiate a CAPA

1. Consider deviations, complaints, audit findings, quality trends, and other relevant quality information as potential sources of corrective or preventive action.
2. The investigation is outside this procedure. The Initiator provides the investigation conclusions and supporting information to the CAPA Owner.
3. Any employee may identify the need for a CAPA; Quality Assurance determines whether a CAPA is warranted, including through the CAPA determinations made under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations. A CAPA is opened when a quality problem has an identified or suspected systemic cause, is recurrent, or presents a significant risk to product quality.
4. The CAPA is recorded in FL-EDMS, and Quality Assurance assigns the CAPA number (format CAPA-<year>-<number>, e.g., CAPA-2026-003). The opening record states the problem, its source, the investigation conclusions, and the CAPA Owner. A CAPA is initiated within 5 business days of the determination that one is needed.
5. Quality Assurance reviews and approves every CAPA opening; the decision is recorded in the CAPA record in FL-EDMS.

### 4.2 Assess the cause and plan actions

1. Review the investigation conclusions and supporting information to assess whether the identified cause is supported by the available evidence.
2. If the cause is not established or remains uncertain, document the uncertainty. The CAPA Owner, together with Quality Assurance, determines and documents that the cause is not established.
3. Quality Assurance routes the issue back to the owner of the source investigation for follow-up. If follow-up cannot establish the cause, the CAPA proceeds against the most likely cause, with the uncertainty and its basis documented in the CAPA record.
4. Distinguish the type of action:
   - A corrective action addresses the cause of a problem that has occurred.
   - A preventive action addresses the cause of a problem that could occur.
   - Fixing the instance of the problem is neither a corrective action nor a preventive action.
5. Define actions that address the identified cause. Consider whether the actions could introduce new risks or increase existing risks. Set the level of effort and documentation in proportion to the risk, uncertainty, importance, and complexity of the issue.
6. Define the effectiveness check in the action plan before implementing the actions. Specify the evidence, the criteria for determining whether the check is effective, and how and when the evidence will be assessed. Every CAPA must close with the effectiveness check defined when the action is planned; do not define or change the check only after the actions are complete. The CAPA record states, for the effectiveness check: the evidence to be reviewed, the objective pass criteria, the check date (a defined period after implementation), and who performs the check.
7. The action plan states each action, its owner, its due date, and the effectiveness check. The CAPA Owner, together with Quality Assurance, completes the investigation, root-cause determination — using a documented root-cause method — and the action plan within 30 calendar days of CAPA initiation. The plan is reviewed by the owner of each affected function and approved by Quality Assurance; the approval, recorded by electronic signature in FL-EDMS, authorizes implementation. Extensions to plan or action due dates require QA Manager approval.

### 4.3 Implement and document actions

1. Carry out the actions according to the action plan after the required approval. Quality Assurance's approval of the action plan, recorded in FL-EDMS, is the authorization to implement.
2. Record completed actions and supporting evidence in the CAPA record.
3. Assess whether implementation introduced new risks or changed existing risks.
4. Document the assessment.
5. The CAPA Owner determines any follow-up the assessment requires.
6. Quality Assurance approves it, and the follow-up is documented in the CAPA record.
7. Before implementing a change to a method or procedure, route the planned change through SOP-QA-004, Change Control, for prospective evaluation and approval. Do not implement the change before that evaluation and approval.
8. After implementation, evaluate whether the quality objectives were achieved and whether the change had an unintended adverse effect on product quality.
9. Record the change and evaluations in the CAPA record.

### 4.4 Verify effectiveness and close the CAPA

1. Perform the effectiveness check defined in the action plan.
2. Compare the results with the criteria specified in that plan.
3. Document the evidence reviewed, the results, and whether the planned criteria were met.
4. If the check does not meet its planned criteria, document the result.
5. The CAPA is reopened, and Quality Assurance decides the next steps — further actions, a revised effectiveness check, further investigation, or a new CAPA — and documents the decision and its rationale in the CAPA record.
6. Close a CAPA only after its planned effectiveness check has been completed and its results documented. Quality Assurance owns the closure decision, and the QA Manager approves closure.
7. Monitor CAPA information and trends as part of oversight of the CAPA system.
8. Quality Assurance reviews CAPA status and trends monthly.
9. Quality Assurance reports overdue CAPAs and adverse trends to the QA Manager, who is the escalation route for overdue actions.

## 5. Records

CAPA records (CAPA-<year>-<number>), including the opening record, investigation and root-cause documentation, action plans and approvals, implementation evidence, effectiveness checks, and closure approvals, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
