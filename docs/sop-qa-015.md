# SOP-QA-015 — Supplier Quality

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how suppliers of materials, components, and services are selected, qualified, audited, and monitored, and how quality responsibilities are agreed with them.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the qualification, auditing, and monitoring of suppliers and contractors whose materials or services affect product quality at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Warehouse and distribution, and the purchasing activity of any function.

Does not cover purchasing of indirect goods and services that cannot affect product quality.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Purchasing**
  - Initiate supplier selection for a new material or service.
  - Purchase only from suppliers on the Approved Supplier List against the agreed specification.
  - Notify Quality Assurance of any supplier or purchasing change before it is made.

- **Supplier Quality Auditor**
  - Evaluate suppliers and contract manufacturers, including laboratories, for GMP compliance at the sites and for the operations being contracted.
  - Quality Assurance assigns auditors, who are trained and independent of the supplier relationship.
  - Quality Assurance approves audit plans and reports.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Quality Assurance is the designated Quality Unit function for supplier approval and oversight.
  - Approve suppliers.
  - Set the depth of qualification and audit in proportion to risk.
  - Approve quality agreements.
  - Oversee the control and review of outsourced activities. The organization remains accountable for the quality of purchased materials and outsourced activities; this accountability is not transferred to a supplier or contractor.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Identify suppliers and assess risk

1. Identify each supplier or contractor whose materials or services can affect product quality.
2. For starting materials, assess the risks posed by the material. Consider its source, manufacturing process, supply-chain complexity, and intended use in the medicinal product. For components and services, apply the same factors, together with the effect a failure would have on product quality.
3. Categorize each supplier under SOP-QA-009, Quality Risk Management, as Critical (materials or services that can directly affect product quality, including raw-material manufacturers, contract laboratories, and contract manufacturers), Major, or Minor.
4. Set the depth of qualification, audit, and ongoing oversight in proportion to the assessed risk.
5. Document the risk assessment and its basis in FL-EDMS.

### 4.2 Qualify the supplier

1. Evaluate whether the supplier can consistently provide the material or service to the agreed quality requirements. For materials, use supporting evidence such as past quality history.
2. Qualification evidence is proportionate to the category: a completed supplier questionnaire and certificate-of-analysis history for Major and Minor suppliers; for Critical suppliers, a quality agreement under section 4.4 and an audit under section 4.3 are also required. Acceptance requires that the evidence shows the supplier can meet the specification and applicable GMP requirements.
3. For critical materials, establish a supplier-evaluation system.
4. Purchase materials against an agreed specification from a supplier approved by Quality Assurance.
5. If the supplier of a critical material is not its manufacturer, record the manufacturer's name and address.
6. Quality Assurance approves each qualification decision.
7. Quality Assurance records the decision and its supporting evidence for each supplier and material approval in FL-EDMS.

### 4.3 Audit suppliers and contractors

1. Evaluate contract manufacturers, including laboratories, for GMP compliance for the specific operations performed at the contract site.
2. Audit manufacturers and distributors of active substances to assess applicable GMP and good distribution practice compliance. Audit Critical suppliers before approval and at least every 3 years.
3. Set further audit intervals through the quality risk management process under SOP-QA-009.
4. For other suppliers, use the documented risk assessment to determine whether an audit is needed and, if so, its scope and timing. Quality Assurance owns the audit decision.
5. Document audit activities and findings.
6. Identify deficiencies in the audit report.
7. Quality Assurance approves the audit report.
8. Track required corrective and preventive actions to completion.
9. Quality Assurance reviews the supplier's responses.
10. Quality Assurance closes the actions.

### 4.4 Approve suppliers and agree quality responsibilities

1. Obtain Quality Assurance approval before purchasing materials from a supplier.
2. Approval status is recorded on the Approved Supplier List in FL-EDMS, maintained by Quality Assurance and accessible to Purchasing and all other users; a supplier not on the list, or listed as suspended or discontinued, is not used.
3. Before outsourced activities begin, establish a written contract or formal agreement that defines the activities, related products or operations, technical arrangements, each party's responsibilities, and communication processes.
4. Identify the activities relevant to each agreement from the outsourced activity itself: the supply-chain tasks, subcontracting, quality activities, purchasing of materials, testing, and release that the contractor will perform, as applicable to that agreement.
5. Document who performs them.
6. Have competent personnel with relevant technical and GMP knowledge develop the technical aspects of the agreement.
7. Ensure both parties agree to the arrangements.
8. Ensure the agreement permits the organization to audit the outsourced activities, including activities performed by mutually agreed subcontractors.
9. Do not allow a contractor to subcontract work without the organization's prior evaluation and approval of the proposed arrangements.
10. Make suitable information about the subcontractor available to the organization.
11. Ensure the agreement requires the contractor to obtain approval before making changes outside the agreed terms that could adversely affect quality.
12. Quality agreements are prepared by Quality Assurance with the owning function.
13. Quality agreements are reviewed by the QA Manager.
14. Quality agreements are signed by both parties.
15. Quality agreements are maintained in FL-EDMS. A supplier of materials that performs no outsourced activity is covered by its Approved Supplier List approval and the purchase specifications, plus a quality agreement where its risk category requires one.

### 4.5 Monitor suppliers and outsourced activities

1. Review records and results related to outsourced activities.
2. Ensure the organization can access records needed to assess product quality and investigate complaints, suspected defects, or suspected falsified products.
3. Monitor supplier performance.
4. Maintain current knowledge of suppliers, supply chains, and associated risks.
5. Quality Assurance reviews each approved supplier's performance annually, covering quality events, certificate-of-analysis failures, delivery and service issues, and audit status.
6. Before accepting received materials, examine each container or group of containers for correct labelling, damage, broken seals, and signs of tampering or contamination.
7. Keep materials quarantined until the applicable examination, sampling, or testing is complete and the materials are released for use. Warehouse receives materials under SOP-QA-023, Warehouse and Distribution Control.
8. Quality Control determines the sampling and testing for each material from its approved specification.
9. Quality Assurance releases the material for use.
10. Investigate damage or other receipt problems that may adversely affect material quality.
11. Record damage or other receipt problems that may adversely affect material quality.
12. Report them to the Quality Control Department the same day; nonconforming material is handled under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations.
13. Review proposed changes to outsourced activities and changes to critical material sources for potential quality impact.
14. Submit each change as a change request under SOP-QA-004, Change Control.
15. Quality Assurance reviews GMP-relevant changes.
16. Quality Assurance approves GMP-relevant changes, and a GMP-relevant change to an outsourced activity is not implemented until the required review and approval are complete.
17. On an escalation trigger — a critical quality event, repeated failures, or loss of a required certification — Quality Assurance decides whether to retain the supplier's approval, add controls (such as increased testing or a for-cause audit), or discontinue use.
18. The decision and its basis are recorded in FL-EDMS.
19. The decision and its basis are communicated to Purchasing and the owning function.

## 5. Records

Supplier quality records, including risk assessments and categorizations, qualification evidence and approvals, the Approved Supplier List, audit plans, reports, and corrective actions, quality agreements, performance reviews, and approval-status decisions, retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
