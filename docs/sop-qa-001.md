# SOP-QA-001 — Good Documentation Practices

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish the requirements for creating, completing, correcting, and retaining GMP records and documents so that records are attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, and accurate.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the creation, completion, correction, review, and retention of GMP records and documents, on paper and in electronic systems, at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory.

Does not cover the approval and version control of documents themselves, which follows SOP-QA-003, Document Control, or the content required in a specific record, which is set in the procedure that creates it.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Record Author**
  - Make and complete records when the activity is performed.
  - Record information accurately and legibly.
  - Identify the person making each entry.
  - Correct errors using the method in this procedure.
  - Sign or initial each entry to show whether you performed, checked, or approved it. You are accountable for the meaning of your signature or initial.

- **Record Reviewer**
  - Review assigned records for accuracy, completeness, and consistency with applicable written procedures.
  - Review relevant electronic audit trails with the associated record.
  - Sign or initial the review to show that it was performed.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Ensure records requiring quality-unit review are routed to the Quality Unit for review and approval before batch release. Quality Assurance and the Quality Control laboratory together form the Quality Unit; the QA Manager is its head.
  - Ensure blank forms are controlled through Document Control or another document control method.
  - Ensure records are retained and can be retrieved for authorized inspection during their retention period. Document Control (within Quality Assurance) sets and maintains retention schedules and retrieval arrangements, approved by the QA Manager.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Record integrity

Make records that are attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, and accurate. Keep them complete, consistent, enduring, and available throughout their retention life.

Make records when the activity is performed. Do not reconstruct records afterwards. Capture all data generated to meet a GMP requirement, including passing, failing, suspect, and apparently erroneous data. Do not discard original data after transcription.

Maintain all information needed to recreate the GMP activity. Keep incomplete or erroneous forms as part of the record, with written justification for their replacement.

Retain production, control, and distribution records associated with a lot for at least 1 year after the lot's expiration date. Retain records for components, containers, closures, and labeling for at least 1 year after expiration of the last lot in which they were used. For any lot without expiration dating, retain these records for 3 years after distribution. Make required records readily available for authorized inspection during the retention period. (21 CFR § 211.180)

Retain all other GMP records, including training records, calibration records, and electronic audit trails, for the periods in the retention schedule maintained by Document Control; retain audit trails at least as long as the records they belong to. Store paper originals in the QA Records Archive (Room 214) and electronic records and scanned copies in the company EDMS (FL-EDMS); Document Control arranges retrieval for authorized inspection.

### 4.2 Creating and completing records

Make handwritten entries clearly, legibly, and indelibly. Complete each entry when the corresponding activity is performed.

Identify each person who performs or checks an activity. Give each signature or initial a defined meaning: performed, checked, or approved. The signer is accountable for that meaning.

If a field on a controlled form does not apply, enter "N/A" and initial and date the entry. A required field is never left blank.

Control blank forms through Document Control in accordance with SOP-QA-003, Document Control.

### 4.3 Correcting paper records

Do not obliterate or remove an original entry. Do not use correction fluid, overwrite an entry, or remove a page or entry.

Correct an error with a single line-through that leaves the original entry legible. The person making the correction must initial and date it. Record the reason for the correction where it is not obvious.

### 4.4 Creating and completing electronic records

Limit electronic record access and changes to authorized individuals. Do not use shared login accounts for actions that must be attributable to an individual. Assign system-administrator rights to personnel independent of those responsible for the record content.

For electronic records within Part 11 scope, apply controls that protect record authenticity and integrity and, when appropriate, confidentiality. Ensure that records can be retrieved accurately throughout their retention period and that accurate, complete copies are available in human-readable and electronic form for inspection and review. Part 11 applies to electronic records created, modified, maintained, archived, retrieved, or transmitted under agency record requirements, and to electronic records submitted under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act or Public Health Service Act, subject to the exclusions stated in Part 11. The QA Manager, with the system owner, determines whether each electronic record or system is within Part 11 scope and whether it is a closed or open system; determinations are recorded in the system inventory maintained by Document Control.

For closed systems within Part 11 scope, validate the system for accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and detection of invalid or altered records. Use secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails to record actions that create, modify, or delete records without obscuring prior information. Retain audit trails at least as long as the associated records and make them available for agency review and copying. Use operational checks for permitted sequencing and authority checks for system use, signatures, access, alterations, and operations, as appropriate.

For open systems within Part 11 scope, apply the closed-system controls as appropriate. Use encryption and appropriate digital-signature standards when needed to ensure authenticity, integrity, and confidentiality from creation to receipt.

Ensure personnel are qualified by education, training, and experience for their assigned electronic-record tasks. Establish written policies that hold individuals accountable for actions performed under electronic signatures.

Review audit trails that capture changes to data associated with a record when reviewing that record. When GMP regulations specify a review frequency, review the audit trail at the same frequency. When no frequency is specified, the QA Manager sets and documents the frequency in the system inventory, using process knowledge and risk assessment, including data criticality, control mechanisms, and impact on product quality; audit trails for laboratory electronic records are reviewed together with the record.

For a signed electronic record, ensure that it shows the signer's printed name, signature date and time, and the meaning of the signature. Link the signature to the record so it cannot be transferred by ordinary means to falsify the record. Make each electronic signature unique to one individual; do not reuse or reassign it. Verify the individual's identity before establishing, assigning, certifying, or sanctioning the signature or any signature element. For nonbiometric electronic signatures, use at least two distinct identification components. During a single, continuous period of controlled system access, require all components for the first signing and at least one component for each later signing. For each later signing, require at least one component that is executable only by, and designed only for use by, the individual.

### 4.5 Reviewing records

Review records for accuracy and completeness against applicable written procedures. Review relevant electronic audit trails with the associated record.

If the review identifies an error, do not approve the record or allow it to proceed to batch release until the error has been fully investigated. Fully investigate unexplained discrepancies before approval or release, under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations. Quality Assurance must review and approve production and control records, including packaging and labeling records, before batch release or distribution. (21 CFR § 211.182)

For personnel in Quality Assurance and the Quality Control laboratory, apply this procedure's applicable record-handling requirements when they review or route production and control records, including packaging and labeling records. This requirement applies to those covered review and routing activities; it does not extend this procedure to production activities outside the functions listed in Section 2.

The analyst completes the record; the QC reviewer performs second-person verification and routes the completed record to Quality Assurance; Quality Assurance reviews and approves production and control records, including packaging and labeling records, before batch release or distribution, as required by 21 CFR § 211.182.

### 4.6 Records handling obligations

Make records attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, and accurate. Keep records complete, consistent, enduring, and available throughout their retention life.

Correct a record error with a single line-through that leaves the original entry legible. The person making the correction must initial and date it, and record the reason where it is not obvious. Do not use correction fluid, overwrite entries, or remove pages or entries.

Make records at the time the activity is performed. Do not reconstruct them afterwards.

Define the meaning of each signature or initial as performed, checked, or approved. The person signing or initialling is accountable for that meaning.

## 5. Records

Retain the completed GMP records governed by this procedure, including associated corrections, reviews, and electronic audit trails, under the applicable retention requirements and arrangements in Section 4.1.
