# SOP-QA-026 — Equipment, Facilities, Utilities, and Systems Qualification

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish the qualification pathway for equipment, facilities, utilities, and supporting systems used in the regulated manufacture and testing of monoclonal antibody drug substances.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the qualification of equipment, facilities, utilities, and supporting systems at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Engineering and Maintenance, Manufacturing, Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory.

This procedure covers qualification from user requirements through design qualification (DQ), factory and site acceptance testing, installation qualification (IQ), operational qualification (OQ), and performance qualification (PQ). It also covers risk-based stage determination, sampling and operating-range justification, requalification triggers and intervals, and qualification records.

Does not cover process validation, cleaning validation, analytical procedure validation, computer system validation, or calibration and preventive maintenance, which have their own procedures (SOP-QA-016, SOP-QA-017, SOP-QC-003, SOP-QA-018, and SOP-QA-020).

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Validation Engineer**
  - Lead qualification planning and execution; prepare qualification protocols and reports; evaluate results against the approved criteria; and recommend stage outcomes. Approve each protocol together with the Equipment Owner.

- **Equipment Owner**
  - Provide the user requirements and technical information for the item, support qualification activities, and approve each protocol together with the Validation Engineer.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Provide quality oversight across the qualification lifecycle: authorize protocol execution, authorize every stage transition, and approve combined or differently sequenced stage approaches and requalification criteria.

Personnel performing qualification work follow approved procedures and are suitably trained under SOP-QA-005, Training and Qualification.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Plan qualification

1. Plan qualification across the lifecycle of the equipment, facility, utility, or system, from definition of requirements through the end of use.
2. Before planning, identify the applicable Validation Master Plan (PLN-001) and follow the qualification system, strategy, and formats it defines.
3. Use quality risk management under SOP-QA-009, Quality Risk Management, to plan qualification activities.
4. Reassess risks when project or operational knowledge changes, and document how the risk assessment informs the qualification.
5. Determine which qualification stages apply to the item. The Validation Engineer, with the Equipment Owner, makes the determination against the stage-applicability criteria in PLN-001 and documents it in the qualification plan.
6. Use suitably trained personnel and approved procedures. Include checks that protect the integrity of qualification data.
7. Before execution, prepare a protocol that identifies the systems, attributes, parameters, test approach, and acceptance criteria; define responsibilities and how results will be recorded and evaluated.
8. The Validation Engineer and the Equipment Owner approve the protocol.
9. Quality Assurance authorizes its execution before execution begins.
10. Review third-party protocols for suitability against internal procedures before approval; supplement them where needed.
11. Document significant protocol changes during execution, including changes to acceptance criteria or operating parameters, as deviations and provide a scientific justification.
12. Record results that do not meet predefined criteria as deviations and investigate them under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations.

### 4.2 Define requirements and design

1. Define requirements in a user requirements specification (URS), a functional specification, or both. Include essential quality requirements and address GMP risks.
2. Use the requirements as a reference throughout the qualification lifecycle.
3. Perform DQ to verify and document that the design meets the requirements and that its compliance with GMP has been assessed.

### 4.3 Conduct factory and site acceptance testing

1. For novel or complex equipment, evaluate it at the vendor before delivery when applicable.
2. The Validation Engineer, with the Equipment Owner, determines whether vendor evaluation and factory acceptance testing (FAT) apply, based on the equipment's novelty and complexity, and records the decision and its basis in the qualification plan.
3. Before installation, confirm at the vendor site, when FAT applies, that the equipment meets the URS or functional specification.
4. Where justified, complete documentation review or testing during FAT without repeating it during IQ or OQ, only when transport and installation do not affect the tested functionality.
5. The Validation Engineer documents the justification.
6. Quality Assurance approves it in the protocol.
7. Conduct site acceptance testing (SAT) after receipt when the qualification plan calls for it, as determined by the Validation Engineer with the Equipment Owner and recorded in the qualification plan.

### 4.4 Verify installation

1. Perform IQ for equipment, facilities, utilities, and systems.
2. Verify that components, instrumentation, equipment, pipework, and services are installed against engineering drawings, specifications, and predefined criteria.
3. Collect supplier operating and working instructions and maintenance requirements.
4. Verify materials of construction.
5. Confirm the calibration status of instrumentation under SOP-QA-020, Calibration and Preventive Maintenance, before proceeding with IQ.
6. Before proceeding from IQ to OQ, obtain formal Quality Assurance authorization for release to the next qualification stage. If IQ and OQ are combined as IOQ, apply the stage-release authorization at the next applicable qualification-stage transition.

### 4.5 Verify operation

1. Normally perform OQ after IQ. IQ and OQ may be performed together as IOQ when appropriate to the equipment's complexity — that is, for equipment of low complexity whose installation and operation can be verified together without loss of evidence.
2. The Validation Engineer and Quality Assurance approve a combined or differently sequenced approach, and the justification is recorded in the protocol.
3. Test that the system operates as designed. Include tests of upper and lower operating limits and worst-case conditions.
4. Following successful OQ, finalize the applicable operating procedures through SOP-QA-003, Document Control.
5. Complete operator training under SOP-QA-005, Training and Qualification, before PQ begins.
6. Before proceeding from OQ to PQ, obtain formal Quality Assurance authorization for release to the next qualification stage.

### 4.6 Demonstrate performance

1. Normally perform PQ after successful IQ and OQ. PQ may be performed with OQ or with process validation where the performance evidence is generated under a representative operating load, including where qualification batches under SOP-QA-016, Process Validation, provide the performance evidence.
2. The Validation Engineer and Quality Assurance approve a combined or differently sequenced approach, and the justification is recorded in the protocol.
3. Select and execute PQ tests for each equipment item, facility, utility, and system in scope. For tests to which production materials apply, use production materials, qualified substitutes, or simulated product shown to behave equivalently under normal operating conditions. Include worst-case batch sizes where applicable. For facilities, utilities, or systems to which production materials or batch sizes do not apply, run the tests under simulated or actual operating loads representative of intended use — for example, utility demand conditions, or facility performance verified with environmental monitoring data under SOP-QC-001, Environmental Monitoring — as defined in the protocol from the user requirements.
4. Justify the sampling frequency used to confirm process control, and cover the intended operating range unless documented development evidence supports a narrower range.
5. The Validation Engineer documents the justification in the protocol, based on the risk assessment under SOP-QA-009 and on development and vendor evidence.
6. Quality Assurance approves it with the protocol.
7. Summarize the results against the acceptance criteria, document the review and conclusions.
8. Make a recommendation on the qualification outcome.
9. Before proceeding to the next qualification stage, obtain formal Quality Assurance authorization. Conditional authorization may be given only when unresolved criteria or deviations have a documented assessment, prepared by the Validation Engineer and accepted by Quality Assurance, showing no significant impact on the next activity.
10. Document the assessment and authorization in the qualification report.

### 4.7 Requalification and changes

1. Evaluate equipment, facilities, utilities, and systems at an appropriate frequency to confirm they remain in a state of control.
2. Quality Assurance and the Validation Engineer determine the requalification frequency based on risk and continued-verification data.
3. If requalification is performed at a set interval, justify the interval, define the evaluation criteria, and assess the possibility of changes over time.
4. Quality Assurance approves the requalification criteria.
5. Use quality risk management to assess planned changes that may affect product quality, reproducibility, qualification, or related systems.
6. Determine whether additional verification, qualification, or requalification is needed.
7. Route changes through SOP-QA-004, Change Control.
8. Review supporting data before final approval.
9. Evaluate change effectiveness after implementation when appropriate.
10. Assess the need for requalification also when any of the following occurs outside a planned change: an adverse trend in monitoring or continued-verification data for the item; a critical deviation, or repeated failures, affecting the item; relocation of the item; a major repair; or an extended out-of-service period.
11. Quality Assurance, with the Validation Engineer, assesses the trigger and determines the requalification need, extent, and criteria.

## 5. Records

Qualification records, including qualification plans and protocols, execution records, deviation records, qualification reports, stage authorizations, and requalification records, retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
