# SOP-RA-001 — Adverse Event Intake and Reporting

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Regulatory Affairs

## 1. Purpose

Establish the process for receiving, documenting, assessing, and reporting adverse experiences associated with the Company's monoclonal antibody drug substance to the FDA under 21 CFR 600.80 and 21 CFR 600.81.

## 2. Scope

Applies to adverse experience intake and reporting at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Regulatory Affairs, Quality Assurance.

This procedure covers intake of adverse experiences from healthcare providers, consumers, scientific literature, internal complaint intake, and other sources; documentation and assessment for reportability; and submission of 15-day Alert reports, periodic reports, follow-up reports, and distribution reports to the FDA. It also covers same-business-day handoff of possible adverse experiences from complaint handling, assessment of biological product deviation reporting under 21 CFR 600.14, and adverse experience record content and retention.

Complaint investigation, product recalls, and clinical trial safety reporting are outside the scope of this procedure.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Regulatory Affairs**
  - Coordinate adverse experience intake, case documentation, assessment, and follow-up. Maintain the reporting calendar in FL-EDMS, prepare and submit reports to FDA, maintain submission confirmations, and report reporting status to the QA Manager monthly.

- **Complaint Handling (Quality Assurance)**
  - Route possible adverse experiences to Regulatory Affairs on the same business day they are identified, under SOP-QA-013, Complaint Handling.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Assess potential biological product deviations and coordinate any required biological product deviation report with Regulatory Affairs. The QA Manager makes the final reportability determination for adverse experiences, including causality conclusions for postmarketing-study cases.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Intake and initial review

1. Promptly review adverse experience information pertaining to the product from any source, domestic or foreign. Sources include commercial marketing, postmarketing clinical investigations and surveillance, scientific literature, unpublished scientific papers, healthcare providers, consumers, and internal complaint intake.
2. An adverse experience is an adverse event associated with use of a biological product in humans, whether or not the product is considered related. This includes events during professional use, overdose, abuse, withdrawal, and failure of expected pharmacological action.
3. Intake channels are the safety mailbox and telephone line monitored by Regulatory Affairs each business day, direct reports to any employee, and the complaint handoff under SOP-QA-013.
4. Any employee who receives a possible adverse experience forwards it to Regulatory Affairs the same business day.
5. Regulatory Affairs records the receipt date — the date any Fictitious Labs personnel first received the information — at intake; that date starts the reporting timelines. Cases are numbered AE-<YYYY>-<NNN> in FL-EDMS, the system of record.
6. Regulatory Affairs monitors the designated scientific literature sources for FL-101 weekly.
7. Before creating a case, Regulatory Affairs checks FL-EDMS for duplicates and for reports previously forwarded to the Company by FDA, matching on patient code, reporter, product, event, and dates.
8. Complaint Handling personnel must hand off possible adverse experiences to Regulatory Affairs on the same business day they are identified.
9. For a person who elects to send adverse experience reports to the final product applicant rather than to FDA, send each report by any appropriate means within 5 calendar days of that person's initial receipt.
10. Keep a record containing a copy of each report, the date it was received, the date it was sent to the applicant, and the applicant's name and address.
11. For each adverse experience, document at intake the reporter identity and contact information, the patient identification code, the suspect product and lot number where known, the event description, and the receipt date.
12. Where any of the minimum information — an identifiable reporter, an identifiable patient, the suspect product, and an event description — is missing, Regulatory Affairs pursues it by documented follow-up under section 4.3.

### 4.2 Case assessment and reportability

1. Assess each adverse experience for seriousness and expectedness against current product labeling. A serious adverse experience results in death, is life-threatening, requires or prolongs inpatient hospitalization, causes persistent or significant disability or incapacity, or results in a congenital anomaly or birth defect. An important medical event may also be serious when appropriate medical judgment indicates that it may jeopardize the patient and may require intervention to prevent one of those outcomes.
2. A life-threatening adverse experience places the patient, in the initial reporter's view, at immediate risk of death from the event as it occurred. An unexpected adverse experience is not listed in current labeling. This includes an event that differs from a listed event in severity or specificity.
3. Regulatory Affairs performs and documents the seriousness and expectedness assessment in the case record.
4. The QA Manager makes the final reportability determination.
5. For an adverse experience from a postmarketing study, a 15-day Alert report is not required unless the Company concludes there is a reasonable possibility that the product caused the event.
6. The QA Manager makes and documents this conclusion with Regulatory Affairs.
7. A case not reported as a 15-day Alert report is assessed for periodic reporting under section 4.3.
8. For information from scientific literature, the 15-day Alert requirement applies only to reports in scientific or medical journals that are case reports or reports from formal clinical trials.
9. Include a copy of the published article with a 15-day Alert report based on literature.
10. Report a serious and unexpected adverse experience as a 15-day Alert report, whether domestic or foreign, as soon as possible and no later than 15 calendar days after initial receipt.
11. Reportability decisions, and the basis for them, are documented in the case record and reviewed by the QA Manager.
12. When information is incomplete or the assessment is uncertain, Regulatory Affairs pursues follow-up information promptly; uncertainty does not extend the 15-calendar-day deadline for a serious and unexpected experience, and the case is reported on the information available.
13. Before applying the biological product deviation criteria below, determine whether the Company holds the biological product license and had control over the product when the deviation occurred. A manufacturing, holding, or distribution step performed by another person under contract is considered under the Company's control when the Company arranged for that step while the product was in its control. Fictitious Labs holds the biologics license for FL-101 and has control over the product through its manufacture and distribution, so this condition is met.
14. If the Company meets this applicability condition, assess whether the event is associated with manufacturing, testing, processing, packing, labeling, storage, holding, or distribution; represents a deviation or unexpected event that may affect product safety, purity, or potency; occurs at the Company's facility or a contracted facility; and involves distributed product. Report as soon as possible and no later than 45 calendar days after the Company, its agent, or another person acting under its control obtains information reasonably suggesting that a reportable event occurred.
15. Use Form FDA-3486.
16. FL-101 is a CBER-regulated product.
17. Send the completed form to the CBER Document Control Center or submit it through CBER's electronic web-based application.
18. Quality Assurance assesses possible deviations and documents the reporting decision, with Regulatory Affairs submitting the report.

### 4.3 FDA adverse experience reports

1. Submit safety report submissions, including individual case safety reports (ICSRs), ICSR attachments, and periodic report descriptive information, in an electronic format that FDA can process, review, and archive. A temporary waiver may be requested in writing for good cause shown.
2. Fictitious Labs is the biologics license applicant and holder for FL-101, a CBER-regulated product, and is the reporting entity. Regulatory Affairs prepares each report.
3. The QA Manager authorizes submission.
4. Regulatory Affairs submits electronically through FDA's electronic submissions gateway to CBER; the submission and FDA's receipt confirmation are documented in the case record in FL-EDMS. Paper submission is used only under a written FDA waiver.
5. For nonvaccine biological product ICSRs, include the applicable patient identification code, age or date of birth, gender, and weight. Include the outcome, event and report dates, event description and medical narrative, adverse experience terms, relevant tests and dates, laboratory data, and relevant patient history. Include the suspect product name, dose, frequency, route, therapy dates, indication, combination-product status, prescription or nonprescription status, whether the event abated after stopping or reducing the dose, whether it reappeared after reintroduction, lot number, expiration date, NDC number or other unique identifier, and concomitant medical products and therapy dates. Include the initial reporter's name, address, telephone number, whether the reporter is a healthcare professional, and the reporter's occupation if applicable. Include the applicant's name and contact office address, telephone number, report source, date received, application number and type, whether the ICSR is a 15-day Alert report, whether it is initial or follow-up, and a unique case identification number that remains the same for initial and subsequent follow-up reports. Do not include an individual patient's name or address; use a unique patient identification code. These fields apply to FL-101 reports; where a field's information is missing, Regulatory Affairs documents the follow-up attempts made to obtain it in the case record.
6. Submit follow-up reports within 15 calendar days after receiving new information or as requested by FDA.
7. Promptly investigate adverse experiences that are the subject of 15-day Alert reports.
8. If additional information cannot be obtained, retain a record of the unsuccessful steps taken to obtain it.
9. Regulatory Affairs collects follow-up information from the reporter.
10. Assesses it against the initial report, and links it to the initial report by the same case identification number.
11. Submit adverse experiences not reported as 15-day Alert reports at quarterly intervals for three years from the biologics license issuance date, then annually. Submit each quarterly report within 30 days after the quarter closes and each annual report within 60 days after the license anniversary date. The first quarter begins on the license issuance date. FDA may change these intervals by written notice.
12. Include in each periodic report a narrative summary and analysis; an analysis of 15-day Alert reports submitted during the interval; a history of actions taken because of adverse experiences; an index of ICSRs submitted in the report; and ICSRs for serious expected and nonserious adverse experiences not previously reported as 15-day Alert reports. Submit ICSRs to FDA once. Except for 15-day Alert information, periodic reporting does not apply to information from postmarketing studies, scientific literature, or foreign marketing experience.
13. Regulatory Affairs maintains the reporting calendar in FL-EDMS covering 15-day, quarterly, annual, and distribution deadlines.
14. Compiles and routes each periodic report for QA Manager review.
15. Submits it as in step 4.
16. If a person named on the product label as a manufacturer, packer, distributor, shared manufacturer, joint manufacturer, or other participant in divided manufacturing submits serious adverse experience reports to the applicant of the final product instead of to FDA, that person must send each report to the applicant within five calendar days of initial receipt.
17. Retain a copy of each report, the date received, the date sent to the applicant, and the applicant's name and address.

### 4.4 Distribution reports

1. Submit distribution information to CBER at six-month intervals, unless FDA gives written notice requiring a different schedule. Include the quantity distributed, including quantities distributed to distributors; bulk lot number; fill lot numbers and total dosage units distributed for each strength or potency; label lot number if different from the fill lot number; labeled expiration date; number of doses in the fill lot or label lot; and the date the lot was released for distribution. Include significant returns of a fill lot or label lot. FDA may require more detailed distribution information. Submit reports electronically in a format FDA can process, review, and archive, unless FDA grants a written temporary waiver for good cause shown.
2. FL-101 is a CBER-regulated product; the six-month interval runs from the biologics license issuance date and is tracked in the reporting calendar in FL-EDMS. Regulatory Affairs prepares each distribution report from the distribution records held under SOP-QA-023, Warehouse and Distribution Control.
3. The QA Manager reviews it.
4. Regulatory Affairs submits it electronically as in section 4.3.

## 5. Records

Maintain records of all adverse experiences known to the applicant, including raw data and related correspondence, for 10 years. Adverse experience records, including case records, assessments, reports, submission confirmations, and distribution reports, are retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices, and in no case for less than the 10 years required by 21 CFR 600.80.
