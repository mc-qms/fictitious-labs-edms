# VMP-001 — Validation Master Plan

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Define the qualification and validation system for the site: the policy, organization, status, and strategy within which facilities, utilities, systems, equipment, processes, and methods used to manufacture and test FL-101 monoclonal antibody drug substance are qualified and validated.

## 2. Scope

Applies site-wide to the facilities, utilities, systems, equipment, and processes used in the manufacture and testing of FL-101 drug substance at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States.

This plan is a controlled plan document, not a step-by-step procedure. It organizes the site's validation family — SOP-QA-016 (Process Validation), SOP-QA-017 (Cleaning Validation), SOP-QA-026 (Equipment, Facilities, Utilities, and Systems Qualification), SOP-QC-003 (Analytical Procedure Validation), SOP-QA-018 (Computer System Validation and Data Integrity), and SOP-QA-020 (Calibration and Preventive Maintenance) — and states how each is invoked. It does not replace any of them; the procedure that governs an activity is followed for that activity.

## 3. Policy

Fictitious Labs plans qualification and validation activities across the lifecycle of facilities, utilities, systems, equipment, processes, and products. Qualification and validation follow a science- and quality-risk-based approach, are performed by suitably trained personnel working to approved procedures, and receive appropriate Quality Assurance oversight throughout the validation lifecycle.

Qualification and validation records are managed so that the integrity of the data obtained is checked and documented. Retrospective validation is not used.

## 4. Organizational structure and responsibilities

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Owns and maintains this plan with the Validation Engineer, and holds the controlled plan in FL-EDMS. Provides quality oversight across the validation lifecycle: approves validation plans, protocols, and reports; authorizes protocol execution; and releases each validation stage.

- **QA Manager**
  - Approves this plan. Releases the process to commercial manufacture on approval of the final qualification report, and approves selection of a continuous or hybrid verification approach under SOP-QA-016.

- **Validation Engineer**
  - Drafts validation plans, protocols, and reports; coordinates execution; evaluates results against the approved criteria; and recommends stage release. Leads qualification planning and execution under SOP-QA-026.

- **Process Owner (Manufacturing)**
  - Owns process knowledge for the process being validated; proposes critical quality attributes, critical process parameters, and acceptance criteria, with their basis; and provides manufacturing participation in validation activities.

- **Equipment Owner**
  - Provides the user requirements and technical information for an item and approves each qualification protocol together with the Validation Engineer.

- **Study Director (Quality Control)**
  - Owns analytical procedure validation under SOP-QC-003 and viral clearance studies under SOP-QA-025: defines the intended use, selects the performance characteristics and acceptance criteria, and owns the protocol and the report for each study.

- **System Owner**
  - Owns each computerized system's validation deliverables, operation, and periodic evaluation under SOP-QA-018.

Qualification or validation work for an item or activity is initiated by the function that owns it — the Equipment Owner for equipment, facilities, utilities, and systems; the Process Owner for a manufacturing process; the Study Director for an analytical procedure; the System Owner for a computerized system — and is executed under the governing procedure in section 10. The Validation Engineer and the Equipment Owner determine which qualification stages apply to an item, against the criteria in section 9, and document the determination in the qualification plan.

## 5. Summary of items covered and qualification and validation status

This plan covers the site's facilities, utilities, systems, equipment, and processes used in the manufacture and testing of FL-101 drug substance. It establishes the framework for:

- Qualification of equipment, facilities, utilities, and supporting systems.
- Process validation and continued process verification.
- Cleaning validation.
- Analytical procedure validation.
- Computer system validation and data integrity.
- Calibration and preventive maintenance.

An item is within the scope of this plan when it is used in the regulated manufacture or testing of FL-101. Facilities, systems, and equipment in scope are those recorded in the FL-EDMS equipment register and system inventory; processes in scope are the FL-101 drug substance manufacturing processes. A new item enters scope when it is introduced for regulated use through change control under SOP-QA-004, Change Control, and the registers are updated by the item's owner and Document Control.

Item-level qualification and validation status is held in FL-EDMS: equipment and facility status in the equipment register, computerized-system status in the system inventory, process validation status in the process's validation records (plans, protocols, reports, and stage-release approvals under SOP-QA-016), and analytical procedure validation status in the procedure's validation records under SOP-QC-003. Continued-verification results are reviewed at least annually, and validated status is summarized for each product in the annual product quality review under SOP-QA-021, Annual Product Quality Review.

## 6. Change control and deviation management

Changes to facilities, equipment, utilities, systems, and processes that may affect product quality are formally documented, and their impact on validated status and the control strategy is assessed, through SOP-QA-004, Change Control. Changes that significantly affect the qualification and validation system described in this plan are likewise managed through SOP-QA-004. Quality risk assessments supporting qualification and validation are repeated when increased knowledge or changes require it, under SOP-QA-009, Quality Risk Management.

Significant changes to an approved protocol during execution, including changes to acceptance criteria or operating parameters, are documented as deviations and scientifically justified. Results that fail to meet predefined acceptance criteria are recorded as deviations and investigated fully under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations: anyone who discovers a deviation reports it the same business day; QA classifies it Critical, Major, or Minor; the investigation is led by an investigator from the owning function together with QA; and QA decides the disposition of affected items. Any implications of a deviation for the qualification or validation outcome are assessed and discussed in the report. Every deviation receives a CAPA determination under SOP-QA-011, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA); Critical and Major deviations require a documented determination.

## 7. Guidance on acceptance criteria

Acceptance criteria are defined in the approved protocol before execution. Criteria are set against the applicable requirements and the intended use of the item or process, derived from the user requirements specification or functional specification, product and process knowledge, development data, and the risk assessment performed under SOP-QA-009. The owner of the activity proposes the criteria with their basis — the Process Owner for process parameters and attributes, the Validation Engineer for qualification tests, the Study Director for analytical performance characteristics — and the criteria are approved with the protocol, under the approval pattern of the governing procedure, before execution begins.

Results are summarized against the predefined criteria in the report. A change to acceptance criteria is scientifically justified and documented: during execution it is handled as a deviation under section 6, and a change to an approved document is made through SOP-QA-004.

## 8. Protocol and report formats

Qualification and validation under this plan use the protocol and report formats defined in this section. A protocol prepared under any procedure in the validation family contains:

- Title, document number, and version, and identification of the item, system, or process.
- Objective and scope.
- References, including this plan and the governing procedure, and the user requirements specification or functional specification where one applies.
- Responsibilities for execution and review.
- A description of the item or process and of the stages or studies to be performed.
- The critical systems, attributes, and parameters to be assessed, with their acceptance criteria and the basis for them.
- The test or analytical methods to be used and their validation status for the intended use.
- The sampling plan and its rationale.
- How results will be recorded and evaluated.
- How deviations will be handled.
- The stage-release or authorization statement that will close the work.

A report contains:

- A summary of the results obtained against the acceptance criteria.
- The deviations that occurred and the assessment of their implications for the outcome.
- The review and conclusions.
- A final recommendation on the qualification or validation outcome.
- The stage authorization: formal release to the next stage, recorded as part of the report approval or as a separate summary authorization in FL-EDMS.

Protocols are approved and execution is authorized as the governing procedure requires: the Validation Engineer and the Equipment Owner approve a qualification protocol and Quality Assurance authorizes it; process validation protocols and reports are approved by Quality Assurance under SOP-QA-016. Release to the next stage is authorized by Quality Assurance. Conditional authorization to proceed may be given only where certain acceptance criteria or deviations have not been fully addressed and a documented assessment, prepared by the Validation Engineer and accepted by Quality Assurance, shows no significant impact on the next activity.

Documents in complex validation projects state their inter-relationship. Where validation protocols or other documentation are supplied by a third party, appropriate site personnel confirm suitability and compliance with internal procedures before approval; vendor protocols may be supplemented with additional documentation or test protocols before use. Qualification documents may be combined where appropriate, for example installation and operational qualification.

## 9. Stage-applicability criteria

Qualification of equipment, facilities, utilities, and systems considers all stages from initial development of the user requirements specification through to the end of use of the item. The Validation Engineer, with the Equipment Owner, determines which stages apply to an item against the criteria below and the risk assessment under SOP-QA-009, and documents the determination in the qualification plan. A combined or differently sequenced approach is approved by the Validation Engineer and Quality Assurance, with the justification documented in the protocol.

- **User requirements specification (URS) and/or functional specification:** required for every new item. The specification defines the essential quality requirements and mitigates GMP risks, and is a point of reference throughout the validation lifecycle.
- **Design qualification (DQ):** required where a design is developed, configured, or selected for the item, to demonstrate and document that the design complies with GMP and meets the requirements of the URS.
- **Factory acceptance testing (FAT) / site acceptance testing (SAT):** applies to novel or complex equipment, as the Validation Engineer with the Equipment Owner decide and record in the qualification plan. Documentation review or testing performed at FAT need not be repeated at IQ or OQ where transport and installation cannot affect the tested functionality, with the justification documented and approved in the protocol.
- **Installation qualification (IQ):** required for every installed item in scope. IQ verifies installation of components, instrumentation, equipment, pipework, and services against engineering drawings, specifications, and predefined criteria; collects supplier operating and working instructions and maintenance requirements; confirms calibration of instrumentation; and verifies materials of construction.
- **Operational qualification (OQ):** required for every item in scope, normally following IQ. IQ and OQ may be combined as an installation/operation qualification (IOQ) for equipment of low complexity whose installation and operation can be verified together without loss of evidence. OQ tests that the item operates as designed, including upper and lower operating limits and worst-case conditions. Completion of a successful OQ allows finalization of operating and cleaning procedures, operator training, and preventive maintenance requirements.
- **Performance qualification (PQ):** required where performance must be demonstrated under representative operating load. PQ normally follows successful IQ and OQ, and may be performed with OQ or with process validation where the performance evidence is generated under representative operating load. For items to which production materials do not apply, PQ tests are run under simulated or actual operating loads representative of intended use, as defined in the protocol. Sampling frequency is justified, and tests cover the intended operating range unless documented development evidence supports a narrower range.

For the other activities this plan organizes, the equivalent staging is set by the governing procedure: process validation follows the lifecycle stages of process design, process qualification, and continued process verification under SOP-QA-016; cleaning validation follows the protocol stages of SOP-QA-017; the performance characteristics assessed for an analytical procedure are selected in its protocol under SOP-QC-003; and the deliverables for a computerized system are scaled to its criticality category under SOP-QA-018.

## 10. Validation strategy

Fictitious Labs applies a lifecycle approach to qualification and validation. Quality risk management under SOP-QA-009 supports planning and decisions throughout; risk assessments are repeated as knowledge increases or changes occur, and the way each assessment supports the work is documented. Checks that protect the integrity of data generated during qualification and validation are incorporated into the work.

Validation work is triggered by: the introduction of a new facility, system, item of equipment, process, or analytical procedure for regulated use; a change assessed under SOP-QA-004 as affecting validated status or the control strategy; a requalification falling due under section 11; or a deviation or CAPA under SOP-QA-010 or SOP-QA-011 that requires validation action.

Each activity is invoked through its governing procedure:

- Invoke SOP-QA-026 for qualification of equipment, facilities, utilities, and supporting systems, planned from requirements definition through the end of use, with the applicable stages documented in the qualification plan.
- Invoke SOP-QA-016 for manufacturing process validation and continued process verification.
- Invoke SOP-QA-017 for cleaning validation of shared and dedicated product-contact equipment.
- Invoke SOP-QC-003 for analytical procedures used for release, stability, and in-process testing.
- Invoke SOP-QA-018 for computerized systems that create, process, or store regulated records.
- Invoke SOP-QA-020 for calibration and preventive maintenance of equipment and instruments, and for the qualification planning of equipment it governs.

Where an item falls within more than one procedure, the procedure governing the primary activity leads, and one qualification or validation plan may coordinate the work: SOP-QA-018 governs the validation of a computerized system while SOP-QA-026 governs the qualification of the equipment that hosts it, and the calibration status of instrumentation is confirmed under SOP-QA-020 in the readiness check before any qualification or validation execution begins.

## 11. Requalification

Equipment, facilities, utilities, and systems are evaluated at an appropriate frequency to confirm they remain in a state of control. Where requalification is performed at a specific time period, the period is justified and the criteria for evaluation are defined; the possibility of small changes over time is assessed.

A requalification assessment is also triggered by: an adverse trend in monitoring or continued-verification data affecting the item; a critical deviation, or repeated failures, affecting the item; relocation of the item; a major repair; or an extended out-of-service period. Quality Assurance, with the Validation Engineer, assesses each trigger and determines the requalification need, frequency, and criteria, from risk and continued-verification data; Quality Assurance approves the requalification criteria.

## 12. Continued verification

Continued process verification is performed under an approved protocol under SOP-QA-016: process performance and product quality are monitored throughout commercial manufacture, trends are evaluated to confirm the process remains in a state of control, and results are reviewed at least annually as an input to the annual product quality review under SOP-QA-021. A significant adverse trend, a major change, or a critical deviation triggers an earlier review. Quality Assurance may change the continued-verification requirements through SOP-QA-004.

Computerized systems are periodically evaluated under SOP-QA-018, at least every 2 years for GMP-critical systems and every 3 years for GMP-supporting systems, and on trigger. Continued-verification protocols, reports, and periodic evaluations are held in FL-EDMS.

## 13. Plan maintenance and review

Quality Assurance maintains this plan with the Validation Engineer and holds the controlled plan in FL-EDMS. The plan is reviewed at least every 2 years under the periodic review of SOP-QA-003, Document Control, and is revised through SOP-QA-004, Change Control, when the qualification and validation system it describes changes significantly. The QA Manager approves this plan and each revision of it.

## 14. Records

This plan; qualification and validation plans, protocols, and reports; stage authorizations; requalification records; and continued-verification protocols and reports, retained in FL-EDMS under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
