# SOP-QC-001 — Environmental Monitoring

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Control

## 1. Purpose

Establish how the microbial and particulate state of controlled environments is monitored, trended, and acted on, so that loss of environmental control is detected before it affects product.

## 2. Scope

Applies to environmental and personnel monitoring in classified and controlled areas at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Control laboratory, Microbiology laboratory, Quality Assurance, Manufacturing.

Covered areas are the classified production suites used for FL-101 drug substance manufacture (areas designated Grade B, C, and D in the facility qualification, including Grade A critical zones where open or sterile operations occur), the QC microbiology laboratory, and controlled but unclassified support areas (gowning, staging, corridors). The monitoring criteria of section 4 apply to each area according to its designated grade; controlled but unclassified areas are monitored at the reduced scope set by the program's risk assessment (section 4.1).

Does not cover monitoring of utilities such as water and compressed gases, which follow their own procedures, or occupational exposure monitoring.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **QC Microbiology (Microbiologist)**
  - Perform the monitoring, support design of the environmental monitoring program, and investigate microbiologically linked events, using appropriate microbiology and process knowledge.

- **Microbiology Supervisor**
  - Own monitoring execution, first review of results, and escalation. Decide, with the operator, whether critical processing pauses or stops on a Grade A alarm, and set the approach where process contaminants could damage particle counters.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Quality Assurance performs the Quality Unit responsibilities for this procedure: approve alert and action levels and program changes, review excursions, and make product-impact decisions together with Quality Control.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Establish the monitoring program

1. Identify the controlled environments, processes, and personnel covered by the program, per the areas listed in section 2.
2. Map each area to the monitoring criteria of this procedure according to its designated grade; controlled but unclassified areas are mapped to the reduced scope set by the risk assessment.
3. Use documented risk assessments to select monitoring locations, frequency, methods, and incubation conditions. Consider process inputs and product, facility and equipment design, critical steps, operations, qualification results, routine data, and typical local flora.
4. Identify locations where microorganisms or particles could affect product quality.
5. QC Microbiology reviews the risk assessment annually and when relevant process or facility changes occur; changes to the program are made through SOP-QA-004, Change Control.
6. Use qualification results and routine monitoring data to establish viable and total-particle alert levels.
7. Review alert levels periodically against trends.
8. Set action limits that may be more stringent than the maximum values below.
9. Alert and action levels are set by QC Microbiology.
10. Approved by Quality Assurance; the approved level tables, by area, grade, monitoring method, and operating state, are held in FL-EDMS and are the only levels results are assessed against. Where no approved level yet exists for an area or method, results are trended and assessed by Quality Assurance until levels are established; a result is never assessed against an unapproved level.
11. Monitor cleanrooms, clean-air equipment, and personnel during critical processing, including equipment setup.
12. Include relevant monitoring during non-operational periods, such as after disinfection, before manufacturing, after batch completion, during shutdown, and in unused associated rooms.
13. Control temperature, relative humidity, and other environmental characteristics within the ranges established in each area's qualification, which support the product, process, personnel, and cleanliness requirements. These characteristics are monitored continuously by the building monitoring system, with alarms.
14. An excursion is assessed by QC Microbiology with Quality Assurance and handled under section 4.4.
15. Distinguish routine environmental monitoring from cleanroom qualification.
16. Use qualification data to inform the routine program.

### 4.2 Monitor airborne particles

1. During critical processing, continuously monitor Grade A locations for particles ≥0.5 µm and ≥5 µm, including during equipment assembly. Use a sampling rate of at least 28 litres (1 ft³) per minute.
2. Compare individual results with limits often enough to support a timely response.
3. Set alarms at alert levels.
4. On a Grade A particle alarm, the operator and the Microbiology Supervisor decide whether critical processing pauses or stops; additional viable monitoring is considered.
5. Quality Assurance assesses product impact, including batches made since the last acceptable result.
6. Consider a similar continuous system for Grade B. If sampling frequency is reduced, ensure it can detect increases in contamination and deterioration of the monitoring system.
7. Set alarms at alert levels.
8. If process contaminants could damage particle counters or create hazards, QC Microbiology identifies the condition.
9. The Microbiology Supervisor, with Quality Assurance, determines the applicable approach, which assures classification before and after exposure, considers increased viable monitoring, and sets simulated-operation monitoring intervals in the program risk assessment.
10. Use the following maximum total-particle limits (particles/m³). Grade D in-operation limits are not predetermined; establish them by risk assessment and routine data where applicable.

| Grade | ≥0.5 µm at rest | ≥0.5 µm in operation | ≥5 µm at rest | ≥5 µm in operation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 3,520 | 3,520 | 29 | 29 |
| B | 3,520 | 352,000 | 29 | 2,930 |
| C | 352,000 | 3,520,000 | 2,930 | 29,300 |
| D | 3,520,000 | Not predetermined | 29,300 | Not predetermined |

11. For at-rest measurements, achieve the limits after the clean-up period established during qualification, with the area unmanned and after operations. The guidance value for the clean-up period is less than 20 minutes; use the qualified, documented period for the area. The qualification-determined clean-up period for each area is documented in the area's qualification record and followed.
12. Investigate consecutive or regular low Grade A counts at ≥5 µm.
13. For areas and operations not covered by the Grade A and Grade B requirements above — Grade C and D areas and controlled but unclassified areas — particle-monitoring methods, locations, and frequencies are those set by the program risk assessment and recorded in the program's monitoring tables in FL-EDMS.

### 4.3 Monitor viable contamination and personnel

1. During aseptic operations, use justified viable monitoring methods, such as settle plates, volumetric air sampling, and glove, gown, and surface sampling. Do not disrupt Grade A or B airflow or operations.
2. Continuously monitor viable contamination in Grade A during all critical processing, including assembly.
3. Consider continuous Grade B monitoring based on risk.
4. Monitor cleanroom and equipment surfaces at the end of operations.
5. Risk-assess personnel monitoring locations, methods, and frequency.
6. Sample personnel periodically, after critical interventions (at minimum, gloves), and on each Grade B exit (gloves and gown).
7. After intervention sampling, replace outer gloves.
8. If gown sampling is required, replace the gown before further activity. Emphasize gown monitoring for manual operations.
9. Expose Grade A and B settle plates during operations and change them after no more than 4 hours.
10. For Grade C and D, limit exposure to 4 hours; set duration and frequency using quality risk management. Do not allow plates to desiccate or sampling to risk manufacturing operations.
11. Use the following viable action limits (CFU). The Grade C and D glove limits are Fictitious Labs' company-set limits, approved by Quality Assurance under section 4.1.

| Grade | Air | Settle plate | Contact plate | Glove |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | No growth | No growth | No growth | No growth |
| B | 10/m³ | 5/4 hours | 5/plate | 5/glove |
| C | 100/m³ | 50/plate | 25/plate | 10/glove |
| D | 200/m³ | 100/plate | 50/plate | 25/glove |

12. Investigate any growth in Grade A.
13. Identify Grade A and B isolates to species level and assess their implications for product and the state of control.
14. For personnel and areas where grade-specific requirements do not apply — controlled but unclassified support areas — monitoring follows the risk-assessment path: settle plates and surface contact plates at the locations and frequencies in the program's monitoring tables, with action levels approved by Quality Assurance under section 4.1.
15. The Microbiology Supervisor selects this path for an area, with Quality Assurance approval, when the area is brought into the program or its classification changes.

### 4.4 Assess results and act on excursions

1. Before assessing results, identify the applicable approved alert and action levels for the area, grade, monitoring method, and operating state from the approved level tables in FL-EDMS; the applicable level is the table entry matching all four.
2. Compare monitoring results with those levels.
3. Assess and follow up alert-level excursions.
4. Investigate action-limit excursions, determine root cause, assess product impact, and determine corrective and preventive actions.
5. For action-limit excursions, include batches manufactured between the time of monitoring and the time the results are reported in the product-impact assessment.
6. Trend monitoring results; do not treat them as records to file without trend assessment.
7. Investigate increasing or consecutive alert or action excursions, recurring isolated action excursions with a common cause, and changes in microbial flora, numbers, or predominance. Pay particular attention to loss-of-control indicators, spore-formers, and moulds.
8. Investigate alert and action excursions for what they indicate about the state of control, including when the product-impact assessment finds no product impact.
9. Record the assessment, investigation, and follow-up.
10. QC Microbiology reviews monitoring and trend data for a batch's manufacturing period as part of product batch certification or release under SOP-QA-006, Batch Record Review and Product Release, and during periodic process review.
11. When data for the time of manufacture are unavailable, review the most recent available data, consider rapid or alternative methods.
12. Document the assessment with Quality Assurance approval.
13. Document and follow up investigations and corrective and preventive actions under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations, and SOP-QA-011, Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA).

### 4.5 Maintain the monitoring program

1. QC Microbiology reviews the program and its trend data annually.
2. Reassess it when results indicate deterioration or when relevant changes affect the monitored area, process, equipment, or monitoring method; changes are assessed and made through SOP-QA-004, Change Control.
3. Test environmental monitoring media for growth promotion before use, using a scientifically justified designated group of reference microorganisms that includes suitable representative local isolates.
4. Perform media quality control testing as the end user unless reliance on supplier or outsourced testing is justified, including consideration of transport and shipping conditions.
5. Monitoring methods and media are qualified or shown suitable by QC Microbiology, with Quality Assurance approval, before use.
6. An alternative method, including one that reports results other than CFU, requires a documented scientific justification and, where possible, correlation to CFU, approved by Quality Assurance.

## 5. Records

Environmental monitoring records, including monitoring results and raw data, excursion assessments and investigations, trend reports, the program risk assessment and monitoring and level tables, and media qualification and growth-promotion records, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
