# SOP-QA-006 — Batch Record Review and Product Release

**Fictitious Labs Inc.** · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-03-02 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

Establish how batch records and associated quality data are reviewed and how the decision to release, reject, or hold a batch is made and recorded.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the review of batch production and control records and the release decision for every batch at Fictitious Labs Inc., a US-based biologics organization manufacturing monoclonal antibody drug substance in the United States, in the following functions: Quality Assurance, Quality Control laboratory, Manufacturing.

Does not cover release of starting materials and components for use in production, which follows the supplier and materials procedures.

## 3. Responsibilities

- **Batch Record Reviewer**
  - Review batch production and control records and associated laboratory data for completeness, compliance with applicable written procedures, and documented results.
  - Identify missing or incomplete entries, unexplained discrepancies, and results that do not meet specifications.
  - Quality Assurance is responsible for reviewing and approving production and control records before release.

- **Manufacturing Supervisor**
  - Resolve discrepancies in the batch record that originate in Manufacturing, by documented correction under SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices, or by initiating a deviation under SOP-QA-010, Handling of Deviations.
  - Escalate to Quality Assurance any discrepancy that cannot be resolved by documented correction.
  - Provide the records and explanations the review requires.

- **Quality Assurance**
  - Quality Assurance is the Quality Unit function designated for this procedure: Quality Assurance performs the review and approval of production and control records before release, holds authority to approve or reject drug products, and makes the release, rejection, or hold decision.

- **QA Manager**
  - Final authority for the rejection of a batch and for the release of a batch carrying an explicitly dispositioned unresolved issue. There is no separate Authorized Person role at Fictitious Labs Inc.; release authority under this procedure sits with Quality Assurance, with the QA Manager as the escalation and final authority described above.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Identify the batch and assemble the review record

1. Identify the batch under review by its batch or lot number.
2. Assemble the batch production and control records and associated laboratory records. Include the batch's production and control information, in-process and laboratory control results, yield information, labeling control records, and records of investigations and sampling.
3. Confirm that the assembled records identify the batch and document its production and control.
4. The reviewer assembles the records listed in the batch's master production and control record, together with the laboratory records for every test in the approved FL-101 specification. The master record's record list, held in FL-EDMS, is the selection rule for the review record.

### 4.2 Review batch production and control records

1. Identify the approved written procedures and master production and control record that apply to the batch. The reviewer identifies them in FL-EDMS: the applicable versions are the versions effective on the batch's manufacturing dates.
2. Compare the batch records with the applicable approved written procedures and master production and control record.
3. Review the documented production and control information, including:
   - Dates and identification of major equipment and lines used.
   - Identification and quantities of components and in-process materials used.
   - In-process and laboratory control results.
   - Actual yield and percentage of theoretical yield at applicable processing stages.
   - Labeling control records and records of packaging and labeling area inspections.
   - Sampling performed; the people who performed, supervised, or checked significant steps; and any investigations.
4. Compare recorded yields with the applicable limits in the master production and control record.
5. Refer any unexplained discrepancy, including a yield outside those limits, for investigation.
6. Identify missing, incomplete, or unexplained entries. Do not treat an unexplained discrepancy or failure to meet a specification as resolved without a documented investigation and conclusion.

### 4.3 Review associated quality data

1. Identify the approved specifications and other quality control criteria that apply to the product and batch. The applicable specification is the approved FL-101 drug substance specification, as cited in the test methods and the master production and control record.
2. Review laboratory results against the applicable specifications and quality control criteria.
3. Confirm that satisfactory conformance to final specifications has been determined before release, including identity and strength of each active ingredient.
4. For a batch required to meet sterility or pyrogen-free requirements, confirm that the applicable laboratory testing was performed and that the results meet the established requirements.
5. For FL-101 drug substance, the review also covers the identity, purity, and concentration results, the endotoxin and bioburden results, and confirmation that the batch was produced from the approved cell bank, with the cell culture records complete.

### 4.4 Resolve discrepancies, investigations, and changes

1. Refer unexplained discrepancies and failures to meet specifications for thorough investigation.
2. Extend the investigation to other batches of the same drug product and to components associated with the discrepancy or failure, as applicable.
3. Quality Assurance decides whether the investigation extends to associated components — required when the discrepancy involves a component, material, or item of equipment shared with other batches.
4. Quality Assurance documents the decision in the investigation record.
5. Document investigation conclusions and follow-up.
6. Disposition each unresolved issue explicitly. Only Quality Assurance may make an explicit disposition.
7. Document the disposition in the deviation or investigation record in FL-EDMS. Release of a batch carrying an explicitly dispositioned unresolved issue requires QA Manager approval.
8. Review changes affecting the batch under SOP-QA-004, Change Control.
9. Confirm that the assessments, checks, and tests the change record requires are complete before release, and that every change affecting the batch is approved and implemented, or carries an approved interim disposition, before release.
10. Before release, close or explicitly disposition all unresolved deviations, investigations, and changes affecting the batch, considering the batch as a whole. An explicit disposition must be supported by an investigation that is sufficiently complete.
11. For an unexpected deviation in a biologic medicinal product, assess the deviation's potential impact on quality, safety, and efficacy through a quality risk management process.
12. Conclude that the impact is negligible.
13. Consider whether the deviation may have an unexpected impact on safety or efficacy.

### 4.5 Make and record the batch decision

1. Keep the batch under quarantine before release. Quality Assurance is responsible for quarantining drug products before release. Safeguards against premature transfer to saleable stock may use physical segregation and labeling or a validated computerized system.
2. Confirm in FL-EDMS that the batch status is Quarantine before proceeding.
3. Quality Assurance reviews and approves production and control records before release.
4. Release the batch only after the required reviews are complete, laboratory determinations show satisfactory conformance to applicable final specifications, and relevant discrepancies, investigations, and changes are closed or explicitly dispositioned.
5. Reject a batch that fails an established specification or other relevant quality control criterion. Quality Assurance has authority to approve or reject drug products. Rejection of a batch requires QA Manager approval.
6. Keep the batch on hold when the review is incomplete, required information is missing, or the decision is not supported. Only Quality Assurance may place or remove a hold. Hold status is controlled in FL-EDMS and by a physical status label on the batch.
7. Quality Assurance documents the removal of a hold in the batch record.
8. Confirm in FL-EDMS that the batch status is no longer Hold before proceeding past a hold.
9. Record the review and the release, rejection, or hold decision in the batch records. The decision is recorded in FL-EDMS and signed in the batch record, stating: the lot number; the reviewer's name, signature, and date; the decision (release, reject, or hold); the decision-maker's name, signature, and date; and the rationale for any rejection, hold, or explicitly dispositioned issue.

## 5. Records

Batch production and control records for each lot, including the assembled review record, the review outcome, and the release, rejection, or hold decision record, retained under the retention requirements of SOP-QA-001, Good Documentation Practices.
